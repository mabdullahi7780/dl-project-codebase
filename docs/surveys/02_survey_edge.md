# Edge & Low-Compute Deployment of Medical Imaging Models

## 1. Introduction and Scope

The clinical value of deep medical-imaging models is increasingly gated not by their peak accuracy but by whether they can be *deployed* where the patients are: in low- and middle-income-country (LMIC) clinics with intermittent power, no GPU, unreliable connectivity, and a mandate to keep patient data on-device. Tuberculosis (TB) chest-X-ray (CXR) screening is the canonical exemplar — the World Health Organization's 2021 consolidated guidelines on TB screening conditionally recommend computer-aided detection (CAD) software *in place of* human readers for interpreting digital CXR in people aged ≥15, the first regulator endorsement of AI substituting for (not merely assisting) a clinician on an imaging task [WHO 2021]. This survey synthesises seven research threads — quantization, pruning/sparsity/token-reduction, knowledge distillation, edge-native architectures, deployment runtimes, real-world TB point-of-care deployment, and federated/privacy-preserving learning — into a single taxonomy of the techniques, trade-offs, and open problems that govern edge deployment of medical-imaging models.

Throughout, we keep a concrete running thread: a **frozen RAD-DINO ViT-B/14 TB-severity model on edge hardware**. RAD-DINO is a DINOv2-class self-supervised ViT-B/14 (86.6M parameters, 768-d embeddings) trained image-only on 838,336 CXRs (~838k; the paper's figure, including ~90k private data) from datasets spanning MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert, NIH-CXR, PadChest and BRAX — note the publicly released checkpoint is instead trained on 882,775 images (~883k) from the five public datasets only; it resizes the shorter image side to 518 px and emits a 37×37 = 1369-token patch grid, and its downstream tasks typically require no encoder fine-tuning [Pérez-García 2024]. This architecture is the prototypical edge-deployment case because the *only* heavy, fixed component is the frozen encoder, while the prediction heads — an ALP-regression MLP and a 49-token spatial-attention cavity head, plus a calibration/uncertainty ladder (kNN, CORAL, deep ensemble, split-conformal) — are trivially cheap and backend-agnostic. The deployment question reduces almost entirely to *how to make one fixed 87M-parameter transformer fast, small, and reliable on GPU-less hardware while preserving its attention-map explanation and uncertainty guarantees*. We use this thread to anchor abstract trade-offs in a recurring concrete decision; the conclusions generalise to any frozen-foundation-encoder medical pipeline.

(Note: RAD-DINO is explicitly released as a research artefact not intended for clinical practice — a fact any deployment must disclose.)

## 2. A Taxonomy of Edge-Deployment Levers

Edge deployment of a transformer-based medical model draws on five interacting compression and systems levers, plus an orthogonal privacy/federation axis. Crucially, these levers are *not* independent: they compose along a Pareto frontier that the literature has only sparsely charted, and several interact adversely (low-bit quantization compounds federated client drift; token merging can corrupt attention-based explanations; differential-privacy noise degrades faster on already-compressed updates).

| Lever | What it removes | Needs training? | Helps latency on GPU-less edge? | Helps memory? | Key risk for a frozen-ViT severity model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PTQ (weight+activation, e.g. RepQ-ViT, PTQ4ViT)** | Numerical precision (FP→INT8/INT4) | No (calibration only) | INT8 yes (ANE A17 Pro/M4, Hexagon, WASM-SIMD); INT4 mostly memory | Yes (~4× at INT8) | Attention/LayerNorm/GELU outliers; subtle cavity-texture signal |
| **QAT / low-bit (Q-ViT, BitMedViT ternary)** | Precision, recovered by retraining | Yes | INT8 yes; sub-INT8 needs special kernels | Yes (up to ~43×) | Heavy training loop; risk to fine spatial signal |
| **Weight-only PTQ (GPTQ, AWQ)** | Weight precision only | No | No (activations stay FP) | Yes | Lowest accuracy risk; no matmul speedup |
| **Token reduction (ToMe, ATM, EViT)** | Input sequence elements (the O(N²) cost) | No (ToMe/ATM) / Yes (DynamicViT) | Yes — attacks the actual 1369-token bottleneck | Yes | Merging mixes spatial provenance → may distort attention map |
| **Structured weight pruning (NViT, head pruning)** | Channels/heads/dims | Yes | Yes (portable wall-clock speedup) | Yes | Mutates backbone weights → breaks frozen-encoder contract |
| **Unstructured / N:M sparsity (LTH, movement, 2:4)** | Individual weights | Yes | N:M only on NVIDIA GPUs; unstructured rarely on edge | Yes | No speedup on ANE/NNAPI/QNN/WASM |
| **Knowledge distillation (DINOv2 self-distill, SEED, DisCo, TinyViT)** | Whole-model capacity → smaller student | Yes (teacher access) | Yes (smaller student) | Yes | Naive logit/feature KD fails for SSL ViTs; explanation fidelity unmeasured |
| **Edge-native architecture swap (MobileViT, EfficientFormer, FastViT)** | Backbone redesign | Yes (re-train) | Yes (MobileNet-speed) | Yes | Abandons frozen RAD-DINO; ViT-vs-CNN ranking is dataset-dependent |

The single most important framing principle, stated repeatedly across the runtime and architecture literature, is that **FLOPs is a poor proxy for on-device latency and energy**: memory-bound operations, thermal throttling under sustained inference, heterogeneous-core scheduling, and framework-level kernel fusion all decouple FLOPs from wall-clock time [Almeida 2021; Vasu 2023 (MobileOne)]. Fair reporting must fix device, OS, framework, precision, and thermal state, and report measured latency, energy, and *peak memory* per backend — the methodology codified by MLPerf Mobile Inference [Janapa Reddi 2022]. This caution recurs in every section below and is the first thing a reviewer or LMIC procurement team needs.

## 3. Quantization

### 3.1 The two regimes and the design axes

Quantization maps high-precision weights and activations to low-bit integers, cutting model size ~4× at INT8 and accelerating inference where hardware exposes integer compute paths. Two regimes dominate. **Post-training quantization (PTQ)** quantizes a frozen, already-trained network using a tiny calibration set — ViT PTQ papers use as few as **32 unlabelled images** — needing no labels, no backprop, and minutes of compute, but degrading at low bit-widths. **Quantization-aware training (QAT)** simulates quantization (fake-quant + straight-through estimator) during fine-tuning, recovering accuracy at 4/2/1-bit at the cost of a full training loop, labels, and gradients. Orthogonal design axes include per-tensor vs per-channel granularity (per-channel is near-mandatory for ViT weights and for the inter-channel variance of LayerNorm outputs), dynamic vs static activation ranges, symmetric vs asymmetric zero-points, mixed precision (sensitive layers kept higher), and weight-only vs weight+activation (WxAy). Weight-only methods (GPTQ, AWQ) shrink memory robustly because weights are well-behaved; W8A8/W4A4 additionally accelerates the matmuls but exposes the hard problem of **activation outliers**.

**For the frozen RAD-DINO thread, PTQ is the natural fit** — the backbone never needs retraining, so the labels-and-gradients cost of QAT is pure overhead unless accuracy demands it.

### 3.2 Why ViTs are harder than CNNs

Standard CNN PTQ recipes drop >1% even at 8-bit on ViTs because of three transformer-specific pathologies: (1) **post-LayerNorm activations** show severe inter-channel variation (a few channels with huge dynamic range); (2) **post-Softmax attention maps** follow a power-law distribution concentrated near zero; (3) **post-GELU activations** are strongly asymmetric. Uniform per-tensor quantization wastes nearly all levels on rare outliers. The seminal ViT-PTQ line addresses each:

- **PTQ4ViT** [Yuan 2022] introduced *twin uniform quantization* (separate scales for the positive/negative regions of post-Softmax and post-GELU values) plus a *Hessian-guided* scale search, reaching <0.5% top-1 drop at W8A8 (ViT-B 84.25%, Swin-B 85.14%) using only 32 calibration images, but ~1.5–3.5% drop at W6A6 and collapse at W4A4 (ViT-B 30.69%).
- **FQ-ViT** [Lin 2022] was the first *fully quantized* ViT (LayerNorm and Softmax included), using a *Power-of-Two Factor (PTF)* per-channel correction for LayerNorm and *Log-Int-Softmax (LIS)* — 4-bit log-domain attention executed with bit-shifts — reaching 84.89% on ViT-L (vs an 85.81% FP32 baseline, a 0.92% drop). This is the recipe for fully-integer hardware (DSP/NPU) with no float path.
- **RepQ-ViT** [Li 2023a] decouples calibration from inference: it calibrates with hardware-unfriendly channel-wise (LayerNorm) and log√2 (Softmax) quantizers, then *reparameterises* the scales into cheap layer-wise / log2 quantizers for deployment, pushing **W4A4 to a usable level** (ViT-B 68.48%, DeiT-B 75.61%, Swin-B 78.32% — vs PTQ4ViT's 30.69/64.39/74.02) and W6A6 to near-lossless (Swin-B 84.57%). This is the most deployable training-free recipe below INT8.
- **I-ViT** [Li 2023b] provides *integer-only* inference (Shiftmax, ShiftGELU, integer LayerNorm) with INT8 accuracy parity and 3.72–4.11× TVM speedup on GPU integer units — important where no float path exists.
- On the QAT side, **Q-ViT** [Li 2022] uses an Information Rectification Module + Distribution-Guided Distillation to reach ~80.9% (ViT-S, +1.0% over FP at the reported setting, ~6.14× theoretical speedup).

### 3.3 LLM methods that transfer

ViTs and LLM transformers share the outlier problem, so LLM PTQ tooling is reusable on a frozen ViT backbone. **SmoothQuant** [Xiao 2023] migrates activation outliers into weights via a per-channel equivalent scaling, enabling lossless W8A8 (1.56× speedup, 2× memory). **GPTQ/OPTQ** [Frantar 2023] is one-shot, Hessian/OBQ-based weight-only 3–4 bit quantization with negligible loss (~3.25–4.5× GPU decode speedup). **AWQ** [Lin 2024], MLSys 2024 best paper, protects the ~1% salient weight channels (identified from activation magnitude) via per-channel scaling, is backprop-free, generalises across modalities including vision encoders, and ships TinyChat for >3× on-device speedup.

### 3.4 Medical-imaging evidence

Medical-imaging-specific evidence is consistent and encouraging for INT8, thinner above it. A PRISMA systematic review of 72 studies establishes quantization as the standard route to move medical models to clinical/edge devices, with INT8 as the workhorse (3–4× compression with typically <1–3% loss when calibration is adequate) and sub-INT8 needing QAT or distillation [Technologies (MDPI) 2026]. *(Verification note: this review is genuine — Technologies 14(1):76, 22 Jan 2026, 72 PRISMA studies — but the source author list was not surfaced; cite with the published author list when finalising.)*

The most directly transferable numbers are CXR-specific: on Chest-XRay14, dynamic-range / float16 / full-int8 quantization yields **2–4× model-size reduction at only 0.0–0.9% mean AUC-ROC decrease**, and integer quantization improves ARM inference latency by **up to 57%** [Abid 2021]. *(Verification correction: this Springer LNNS chapter, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-86261-9_8, DCAI 2021, is authored by Abid A., Sinha P., Harpale A., Gichoya J., Purkayastha S. — it must not be attributed to "Bishshoy Das et al.", a misattribution flagged in verification.)*

At the extreme low-bit edge, **BitMedViT** [Walczak 2025] ternary-quantises a medical ViT (W2 BitNet-1.58 weights + low-precision/INT8 activations) with distillation, scoring 86% vs an 89% FP teacher (3% drop) on MedMNIST (which includes ChestMNIST and PneumoniaMNIST), at 43× model-size and 39× memory-traffic reduction, and 16.88 ms on a Jetson Orin Nano (21.7× faster). *(Verification correction: the paper reports up to ~41× energy efficiency at 183.62 GOPs/J — an earlier "22×" figure was a misattribution and should not be cited; "low-precision activations" is the safe phrasing.)* This is the closest published analogue to a medical ViT deployed on embedded hardware.

### 3.5 Hardware support and caveats

INT8 is universally accelerated: the **Apple Neural Engine** runs W8A8 on the int8-int8 path of A17 Pro/M4, and Core ML Tools supports 8-bit and 4-bit weights with per_tensor/per_channel/per_block granularity plus palettization [Apple Core ML Tools 2024; Apple WWDC24]. The **TFLite/LiteRT + Qualcomm Hexagon DSP** path runs 8-bit symmetric models at 3–25× speedups [TensorFlow Hexagon delegate]. *(Verification note: prefer the GitHub README / 2019 blog as the stable source — the original tensorflow.org/lite/performance/hexagon_delegate page now redirects.)* **ONNX Runtime Web** offers q8/q4 on WASM (SIMD INT8 ≈ 2–3× over FP32) and WebGPU. Critically, INT4/INT2/binary are accelerated only on a subset of accelerators; on commodity CPU they mainly save memory, not latency, and OpenVINO/NNCF documentation warns that ViT INT8 PTQ is fragile (post-GELU activations, batch>1 not recommended for transformers). The 2025 mobile-ViT latency study makes the warning concrete: INT8 quantization can *degrade* latency on some cores (a QNNPACK GELU pathology of 2.85×, channel-multiple-of-32 cliffs) — **quantization is not free on mobile** [Z. Li 2025].

For the frozen RAD-DINO thread, the recommended path is training-free and INT8-first: W8A8 with per-channel weights and RepQ-ViT/SmoothQuant-style handling of the three trouble spots, using 32 in-domain CXR calibration images. Below INT8, prefer RepQ-ViT (training-free W4A4/W6A6) or AWQ/GPTQ weight-only INT4 for pure memory reduction. A pragmatic hybrid is to **keep the backbone INT8 and run the cheap heads in FP**, since quantizing the heads buys little and risks regression/calibration error on the continuous severity output.

## 4. Pruning, Sparsity, and Token Reduction

Two largely orthogonal families compress a ViT: **weight pruning/sparsity** (remove parameters) and **token reduction / adaptive computation** (remove or merge input sequence elements). For a ViT whose attention cost is O(N²·d) and MLP cost O(N·d²), token reduction is uniquely attractive when N is large — exactly the regime of 518×518 at patch-14 (37×37 = 1369 tokens). This is the section most directly aligned with the frozen-encoder bottleneck.

### 4.1 Weight pruning and sparsity

**Unstructured magnitude pruning** yields high sparsity with minimal accuracy loss but irregular sparsity that commodity hardware cannot accelerate. The **Lottery Ticket Hypothesis** [Frankle 2019] showed dense nets contain sparse "winning ticket" subnetworks trainable from the original initialisation, but found only via costly iterative magnitude pruning + rewinding. **Movement pruning** [Sanh 2020] is first-order — it prunes weights moving toward zero during fine-tuning — and is strictly better than magnitude pruning in the transfer-learning regime, reaching ~3% of parameters with distillation. **Head pruning** [Michel 2019] showed 20–40% of attention heads are removable with negligible loss, some layers reducible to one head, but aggressive removal collapses accuracy without retraining.

Structured pruning is the hardware-friendly variant. **NViT** [Yang 2023] uses global Hessian-aware saliency + latency regularization to prune DeiT-Base to 2.6× FLOPs, 5.1× params, and **1.9× real wall-clock speedup** near-losslessly. **SViTE/S²ViTE** [Chen 2021] jointly trains sparse connectivity and token selection, reporting a sparse DeiT-Small that *gains* 0.28% top-1 while cutting 49.32% FLOPs (4.40% runtime) — sparsity as a "free lunch."

**N:M structured sparsity** [Mishra 2021] is the hardware-aware sweet spot: the **2:4** pattern is accelerated ~2× by NVIDIA Ampere/Hopper Sparse Tensor Cores via 2-bit metadata. Crucially, **2:4 buys nothing on Apple ANE, NNAPI, QNN, or WASM/WebGPU** — these backends lack structured-sparse GEMM kernels — so N:M is a server/desktop-GPU lever, not an LMIC-edge lever.

### 4.2 Token reduction and adaptive computation

This is the right tool when the bottleneck is token count on a *frozen* backbone, and it splits into needs-training and training-free methods:

- **Token Merging (ToMe)** [Bolya 2023] — the seminal training-free method: bipartite soft matching merges the k most-similar tokens (cosine similarity of attention keys) between the attention and MLP blocks each layer. Off-the-shelf it **doubles throughput of ViT-L@512 and ViT-H@518 with only 0.2–0.3% accuracy drop**, and can also be applied during training. This is the closest match to a frozen-encoder deployment.
- **Adaptive Token Merging (ATM)** [Lee 2025] — newest training-free SOTA: per-layer/per-image similarity thresholds; >30% FLOPs off DeiT-T/S with *zero* accuracy loss, outperforming all training-free methods. *(Verification note: the additional claims that ATM was "validated on self-supervised MAE ViTs" and "beats EViT/ToMe" specifically are not confirmable from the abstract and should be treated as plausible-but-unverified; ToMe is the standard baseline.)*
- **EViT** [Liang 2022] — ranks tokens via CLS-token attention, keeps top-k by a *keep-rate* (0.9→0.5), fuses inattentive tokens into one. DeiT-S +50% inference speed at 0.3% loss; works training-free but is best with a short fine-tune. *(Verification note: the "keep-rate 0.7 → ~79.4%" sub-number could not be independently confirmed from the abstract.)*
- **DynamicViT** [Rao 2021] — lightweight prediction modules + Gumbel-Softmax + attention masking hierarchically prune ~66% of tokens, cutting 31–37% FLOPs and improving throughput >40% within 0.5% accuracy. **Needs training.**
- **A-ViT** [Yin 2022] — per-token adaptive halting with *zero* added parameters, 62% throughput (DeiT-Tiny) / 38% (DeiT-Small) at ~0.3% loss. **Needs training**, and emits *variable* token counts. *(Note: this is arXiv:2112.07658, confusingly titled "AdaViT" on arXiv — distinct from the Meng et al. AdaViT below.)*
- **AdaViT** [Meng 2022] — a learned decision network selects which patches, heads, and blocks to use per input, trained end-to-end (arXiv:2111.15668).
- **SPViT** [Kong 2022] — latency-aware soft token pruning into a "package token" with device-specific latency budgets — explicitly an edge-device framework.

Medical-specific evidence is thinner but converging. **PrATo** [Dutta 2025] prunes 35–55% of tokens in a ViT medical-segmentation pipeline, explicitly motivated by real-time diagnosis in resource-constrained clinics. **SqueezeViT** [Maurya 2026] is a CXR-purpose-built token-squeezing ViT with 43.2% fewer parameters than MobileViT and up to 95.4% vs other SOTA. The architectural alternative **EfficientViT** [Cai 2023] replaces softmax with multi-scale linear attention for a global receptive field at large speedups across mobile CPU/edge GPU.

### 4.3 The accuracy–latency–training–explainability trade-off

Training-free methods (ToMe, ATM, naive EViT) give 1.5–2× throughput at ≤0.3–0.5% accuracy loss with no labels and no GPU-hours — ideal when the backbone is frozen. Trained methods (DynamicViT, A-ViT, NViT) push further (2–2.6× FLOPs) but cost a fine-tuning loop and risk overfitting on small medical sets; their *dynamic token counts often cannot be exported to static-graph mobile runtimes* (Core ML/TFLite/ONNX) at all. Merging consistently beats hard pruning at equal token budget in early layers because it summarises rather than discards; hard pruning wins in late layers once tokens are dissimilar — motivating hybrid prune+merge designs.

For the frozen RAD-DINO thread, **training-free token merging inside the ViT-B (ToMe / ATM) is the single highest-leverage optimisation**: it attacks the actual 1369-token O(N²) cost, which average-pooling-to-49 *after* the encoder does not. A standing caution that the literature does not resolve — and which the project must measure — is that merging mixes the spatial provenance of tokens, potentially corrupting the 49-token spatial-attention map that *is* the clinical explanation; ToMe-style proportional-attention bookkeeping is the safest choice for keeping that map interpretable. Structured pruning (NViT) and 2:4 sparsity are largely out of scope for a frozen encoder on ANE/NNAPI/QNN/WASM targets.

## 5. Knowledge Distillation

Knowledge distillation (KD) transfers the behaviour of a large teacher into a compact student, organised along three axes: *what knowledge* (response/feature/relation), *online vs offline*, and *self-distillation*. For ViTs, which lack convolutional inductive bias and overfit on small data, KD is especially valuable — and for distilling a *self-supervised foundation encoder* like RAD-DINO, the choice of objective is decisive.

### 5.1 The knowledge-type axis

- **Response-based** KD [Hinton 2015] matches temperature-softened teacher softmax via KL, transferring "dark knowledge" of inter-class relations. *(Venue note: the canonical venue is the NIPS 2014 Deep Learning Workshop.)*
- **Feature-based** KD: FitNets [Romero 2015] regress intermediate "hints"; Attention Transfer [Zagoruyko 2017] matches pooled spatial attention maps — directly relevant to preserving attention-based explanations.
- **Relation-based** KD: RKD [Park 2019] preserves distance-wise and angle-wise relations among instances; CRD [Tian 2020] maximises mutual information between teacher/student embeddings contrastively. *(Verification correction: the correct arXiv for CRD is 1910.10699; a previously cited 1810.05236 is an unrelated paper.)*
- **Online/mutual**: Deep Mutual Learning [Zhang 2018] trains a cohort of peers that can beat a fixed teacher. **Self-distillation**: Born-Again Networks [Furlanello 2018] iterate a same-capacity student that surpasses its teacher; Be Your Own Teacher [Zhang 2019] distills deep layers into shallow ones with no extra inference cost.

### 5.2 Transformer-specific KD

**DeiT** [Touvron 2021] introduced the *distillation token* and two robust empirical findings: (i) **hard distillation** (teacher argmax) beats soft KL for transformers (DeiT-B hard distillation 83.0% vs the 81.8% no-distillation baseline at 224², with soft distillation sitting at roughly the no-distillation level); (ii) a **CNN teacher (RegNetY-16GF) beats a transformer teacher**, because convnet inductive biases transfer through distillation. Distilled DeiT-B reaches 83.4%. **TinyViT** [Wu 2022] does fast pretraining distillation, caching sparsified teacher logits to disk so a small student inherits massive-scale pretraining cheaply — 84.8% at 21M params, rivalling Swin-B (4.2× fewer params); this caching trick makes distilling over a large CXR corpus computationally tractable. A 2024 NeurIPS result shows that copying only a teacher ViT's **attention maps** recovers most of the benefit of pretraining [A. Li 2024], strong evidence that attention patterns carry the transferable knowledge — and that distilling RAD-DINO's attention could preserve *both* accuracy and the explanation map.

**Cross-architecture KD (ViT→CNN)** is hard: transferability across architectures is typically <0.55 vs 0.6–0.7 for homologous pairs. [Liu 2022] adds a Partially-Cross-Attention projector and a Group-wise Linear projector to lift a ResNet50×2 student under a Swin-L teacher. *(Verification note: the specific figures — <0.55 transferability and 81.39% ImageNet — could not be independently re-verified because the ACCV PDF was access-blocked; treat as plausible-but-unconfirmed.)* This matters because the cheapest edge student is often a CNN, with excellent NPU/TFLite/Core ML support.

### 5.3 Distilling self-supervised / foundation ViTs — the RAD-DINO regime

This is the regime closest to the project. **DINOv2** [Oquab 2023] does *not* train small models from scratch — it distills a frozen ViT-g teacher (~1.1B params) into ViT-S/B/L students by re-running the SSL (iBOT) objective; distilled students beat from-scratch on all 12 benchmarks (distilled ViT-L 86.3% linear vs 86.5% teacher). Two seminal works show *why* naive SSL fails on tiny nets and how to fix it: **SEED** [Fang 2021] — MoCo-v2 on MobileNetV3-Large gives only 36.3% linear probe; distilling the teacher's *similarity distribution over a queue* lifts it to 68.2% (+31.9 pts), EfficientNet-B0 42.2%→67.6%. **DisCo** [Gao 2022] — constrain the student's *final embedding* to match the teacher's, fixing a "distilling bottleneck" with a train-only enlarged MLP (discarded at deployment, zero inference cost), giving EfficientNet-B0 strong accuracy at 9.4% of a ResNet-101 teacher's params. The consistent lesson: **relational/embedding distillation of SSL features, not logit KD, is what preserves downstream accuracy in tiny students.**

A practical warning for distilling RAD-DINO's patch grid: naive feature-map MSE often fails for ViTs because their representations are (per-image) low-rank, requiring feature-lifting and width-alignment [Tian 2025]. *(Verification correction: the canonical title is "From Per-Image Low-Rank to Encoding Mismatch…", authors Huiyuan Tian, Bonan Xu, Shijian Li, accepted ICML 2026.)* ViTKD [Yang 2022] gives the complementary recipe: mimic the teacher's shallow feature maps and *generate* (rather than mimic) deep features.

### 5.4 Medical-imaging KD

CXR-specific evidence concentrates the lessons. **Park 2022** (Nature Communications) distills a large self-supervised CXR ViT into smaller students with improved robustness, gains maximised in low-resource settings — the canonical "train-big/distill-small" LMIC pattern. A 2025 study distills a SWAV self-supervised teacher into a ResNet-18 student reaching ~97.34% CXR accuracy at far lower GFLOPs [Discover Computing 2025]. **MedAlmighty** [Ren 2025] distills a frozen DINOv2 teacher into a compact ResNet across all 12 MedMNIST modalities and explicitly finds that *frozen-backbone + linear-probe underperforms on domain-specific tasks*, motivating distillation to a smaller specialised model — an evidence-based caution directly relevant to a frozen RAD-DINO + linear head.

**Explainable medical KD** [Mir 2025] distills VGG19/Visformer/AutoFormer teachers into an OFA-595 student on COVID-QU-Ex CXRs (96.8% acc) and LC25000, verifying via Score-CAM that the student attends to the same pathological zones — but *only qualitatively*, exposing a clear gap. Lightweight TB CXR models without KD set the feasibility bar: a GhostNet+MobileViT hybrid reaches 99.52% on a 7,000-image set and 99.17% on a 1,600-image TBX11K subset at 7.73M params, 282M FLOPs, ~65 ms/image [Owda 2025]. *(Verification note: the 99.17% is on a 1,600-image subset, not the full TBX11K benchmark.)*

For the frozen RAD-DINO thread, the literature points to a defensible plan: re-run the DINOv2 iBOT objective with the frozen teacher to train a ViT-S/14 student (lowest-risk, empirically beats from-scratch); use **relational similarity distillation (SEED) or embedding matching (DisCo)** plus per-patch feature/attention matching to preserve the 49-token grid; add an attention-matching term to retain the explanation; and re-fit the calibration ladder on student features — validating that conformal coverage and CORAL alignment still hold.

## 6. Efficient / Edge-Native Architectures

Two design lineages converge on "MobileNet-speed" inference: **reparameterizable CNNs** that collapse a multi-branch training graph into a single linear inference graph, and **hybrid CNN-transformer / efficient-attention models** that tame attention's quadratic cost. A third axis — NAS-designed mobile CNNs — remains the most deployment-hardened. This section is relevant when the project considers replacing rather than merely compressing RAD-DINO.

### 6.1 Reparameterization — the single most important idea for true edge speed

**RepVGG** [Ding 2021] trains a multi-branch block (3×3 + 1×1 + identity) and algebraically folds it into one 3×3 conv at inference, reaching >80% ImageNet top-1 as a plain stack while running ~83% faster than ResNet-50 on a 1080Ti. **MobileOne** [Vasu 2023a] applies this to a mobile backbone, hitting **75.9% top-1 at <1 ms on iPhone 12**, 38× faster than MobileFormer, and is the paper that most forcefully argues FLOPs/params correlate poorly with on-device latency. **FastViT** [Vasu 2023b] generalises the trick to token-mixing via **RepMixer**, which removes skip connections at inference to cut memory traffic: FastViT-T8 reaches 76.2% @ 0.8 ms, MA36 83.9% @ 4.6 ms on iPhone 12 Pro, 4.9× faster than EfficientNet and 1.9× faster than ConvNeXt at matched accuracy. The catch for the project: pure reparam CNNs emit a conv feature map, not a native patch-token attention grid.

### 6.2 Hybrid efficient-attention transformers — the token-grid-preserving family

These are the most relevant family for a patch-grid head. **MobileViT** [Mehta 2022] reaches 78.4% at ~6M params (+3.2% over MobileNetV3); **MobileViTv2** [Mehta 2022b] swaps O(N²) attention for **separable (linear) self-attention**, running ~3.2× faster on mobile; **MobileViTv3** [Wadekar 2022] simplifies the fusion block (3×3→1×1) for +1–2% at small scales. **EdgeViT** [Pan 2022] introduces a local-global-local (LGL) bottleneck applying attention only over a sparse set of delegate tokens, and is notable for reporting on-device latency *and* energy. **EdgeNeXt** [Maaz 2022] uses split depth-wise transpose attention (attention across channels, not tokens): a 1.3M model at 71.2% top-1, +2.2% over MobileViT at 28% fewer FLOPs. **EfficientFormer** [Y. Li 2022] showed a pure-transformer dimension-consistent design can match MobileNet speed: L1 79.2% @ 1.6 ms on iPhone 12. **EfficientFormerV2** [Y. Li 2023] uses joint latency+param NAS to give S0 76.2% @ 3.5M/0.9 ms, S2 82.0% @ 12.6M/1.6 ms, L 83.5% @ 26.1M/2.7 ms — concrete edge-sized student bodies. **LeViT** [Graham 2021] is the CPU-oriented option (convolutional embeddings, BatchNorm, attention bias): 5× faster than EfficientNet on CPU at 80% top-1, directly relevant to GPU-less clinics. **Castling-ViT** [You 2023] trains with both softmax and linear-angular attention but switches to *linear-only at inference*, giving up to 40% MACs reduction at iso-accuracy — removing the quadratic term that dominates 518²-token inputs.

### 6.3 NAS CNNs — the deployment-hardened baseline

**MobileNetV2** [Sandler 2018]: inverted residuals + linear bottlenecks, 72.0% @ 3.4M. **MobileNetV3** [Howard 2019]: hardware-aware NAS + NetAdapt + h-swish. *(Verification correction: the original abstract states MobileNetV3-Large is +3.2% more accurate at −20% latency over V2 (an earlier "−15%" latency figure is incorrect), and MobileNetV3-Small is +6.6% more accurate at comparable latency.)* **EfficientNet** [Tan 2019] introduced compound scaling; **EfficientNet-Lite** [Liu 2020] strips squeeze-excitation and replaces swish with ReLU6 *specifically because they quantize poorly*, with integer-only Lite4 reaching 80.4% top-1 at ~30 ms on a Pixel 4 CPU — the canonical recipe for quantizable edge backbones.

### 6.4 The cross-cutting trade-off and a key medical caution

(a) FLOPs ≠ latency — quote on-device ms. (b) Reparam models are fastest but pure-CNN. (c) Hybrid/transformer models preserve a token grid (natural fit for a 7×7 spatial head) but their dynamic attention, LayerNorm, and GELU can be slower or unsupported on INT8 mobile paths; EfficientNet-Lite's ablation (drop SE/swish for quantizability) is the canonical warning. (d) Linear/separable-attention variants (MobileViTv2, EdgeViT, Castling-ViT) directly reduce the 518²-token quadratic cost.

Two medical findings sharpen architecture selection. First, ViT-vs-CNN ranking is **dataset-dependent at edge scale**: on pneumonia CXR, MobileViT-Small reached 97.50% accuracy / 0.9664 F1, while EfficientFormerV2-S0 was *outperformed by both* MobileNetV3-Large and MobileViT-Small [medRxiv 2024] — so the student must be selected empirically on the target task, not by ImageNet rank. Second, the resolution mismatch: edge backbones are tuned for 224–256 px, but RAD-DINO needs 518 px for its 37×37 grid; how to obtain a high-resolution token grid cheaply on edge is under-explored.

For the frozen RAD-DINO thread, the architecture literature argues *distill rather than redesign* — keep the 518→37×37→49-token contract and distill into a token-producing student (EfficientFormerV2-S0 or FastViT-SA12 are realistic sizes, ~30–200× fewer params than ViT-B) — and, if replacing the encoder, pick a token-grid backbone (MobileViTv2/v3, EfficientFormerV2, EdgeViT) over a pure reparam CNN so the spatial cavity head transfers unchanged.

## 7. Deployment Runtimes and Toolchains

Deploying a frozen 87M-parameter ViT-B to GPU-less hardware is dominated by one cost centre — the encoder's attention and MLP blocks — so the deployment question is *which runtime executes a transformer encoder fastest, smallest, and most reliably on each target*, and how to export a frozen PyTorch ViT without breaking attention or dynamic shapes. A clean architectural fact helps: because only the encoder is heavy, one exports the encoder *once per target* and runs the cheap heads natively.

### 7.1 Apple (iPad / Core ML / ANE)

Core ML + `coremltools` is the only path to the Apple Neural Engine, which is *transformer-hostile by default*: its last tensor axis must be 64-byte aligned, so a naive `(B,S,C)` layout wastes 32× (16-bit) to 64× (8-bit) memory. Apple's guidance prescribes four rewrites: (1) a `(B,C,1,S)` channels-first 4D layout with `nn.Linear`→`nn.Conv2d 1×1`; (2) single-head attention chunking for L2 residency; (3) reshape/transpose-free attention; (4) bandwidth-awareness for short sequences — yielding up to 10× faster inference and 14× lower peak memory (DistilBERT 3.47 ms at seq-128 on iPhone 13) [Apple ANE Transformers 2022]. *(Verification note: the 10×/14× figures are for higher seq-len/batch; at seq-128/batch-1 the DistilBERT speedup is 2.84× — the two regimes should not be conflated.)* For ViTs specifically, the ANE caps at 5D tensors (forcing sequential height/width window factoring) and relative position embeddings should be swapped for single-head RPE or LePE [Apple ViT-ANE 2023]. The critical gotcha: a stock-exported RAD-DINO compiles to Core ML but lands on GPU/CPU, *not* ANE, unless attention is rewritten — a one-time surgical export acceptable for a frozen encoder. Core ML supports INT8/INT4 weights and palettization, but activation+weight INT8 only accelerates the Neural Engine on A17 Pro/M4 [Apple Core ML Tools 2024].

### 7.2 Android (LiteRT/TFLite, NNAPI, Qualcomm QNN/Hexagon)

TFLite is now **LiteRT** under Google AI Edge. The portable default is the **XNNPACK CPU delegate**; GPU and NPU delegates are optional and *silently fall back to XNNPACK* if unsupported. NPU acceleration comes through the **Qualcomm QNN delegate** targeting the Hexagon Tensor Processor [Google AI Edge / Qualcomm 2024]. Qualcomm's stack supports INT4/INT8/INT16/FP16 with dedicated transformer/MHA acceleration up to 4.35× faster [Qualcomm 2024]. *(Verification note: the 4.35× is a MobileBERT-specific benchmark, not a general attention-acceleration figure.)* As noted in §3.5, INT8 can paradoxically *slow* some ViT ops on mobile [Z. Li 2025], so it must be benchmarked, not assumed.

### 7.3 Browser (ONNX Runtime Web, transformers.js, WebNN)

The web path is the most deployment-friendly for LMIC clinics — zero install, privacy-by-construction ("data never leaves the tab"). **ONNX Runtime Web** with the WebGPU EP gives large speedups over WASM (Segment-Anything encoder 19×, decoder 3.8×) and supports FP16 on Chrome/Edge 121+ [Microsoft ORT-Web 2024]. WASM SIMD + INT8 is the universal CPU fallback (~2–4× over scalar FP32). **transformers.js v3** [Hugging Face 2024] wraps ORT-Web, exposes `device: webgpu|wasm|webnn` and dtypes fp32/fp16/q8/int8/q4, and ships 120 architectures with 1,200+ pre-converted models — though a DINOv2/RAD-DINO model needs a custom ONNX export. **WebNN** [W3C 2026] reached Candidate Recommendation (Jan 2026) but remains preview-only and flag-gated — too immature for a clinical tool; ORT-Web WebGPU is the production browser choice.

### 7.4 Cross-platform / CPU (ExecuTorch, ONNX Runtime, OpenVINO, NCNN/MNN)

For commodity mini-PCs, **ExecuTorch** [PyTorch ExecuTorch 2024] is the most direct path from a frozen PyTorch RAD-DINO: `torch.export` → `.pte`, lowered to XNNPACK, with PT2E INT8 and a ~50 KB runtime. *(Verification correction: an "INT8 ~1.83× on ARM (SqueezeSAM)" figure circulating with this docs URL is actually from a separate Arm SME2/KleidiAI blog and should be re-sourced.)* **ONNX Runtime** (CPU, OpenVINO, DirectML, TVM EPs) is the universal export-once-target-many hub; **OpenVINO + NNCF** is the mature x86 INT8 PTQ choice but warns ViT INT8 is fragile. **NCNN/MNN** are tiny ARM-NEON engines with ViT support.

For the frozen RAD-DINO thread, the per-target plan is: rewrite attention for ANE on iPad; ship XNNPACK CPU as guaranteed fallback plus the QNN/Hexagon delegate on Snapdragon; serve one ONNX graph via ORT-Web WebGPU (WASM-INT8 fallback) in the browser; and use ExecuTorch+XNNPACK or ONNX+OpenVINO on mini-PCs. Because the input is fixed at 518×518, shapes can be frozen at export, sidestepping the dynamic-shape op-coverage problems that plague ViT conversion. The spatial-attention explanation and conformal intervals, computed on the frozen features, transfer to any backend that faithfully reproduces the 37×37→7×7 token grid.

## 8. Real-World Medical / TB Edge Deployment and Point-of-Care AI

TB CXR screening is the most mature, regulator-endorsed example of deploying medical-imaging AI at the point of care in LMICs, and is the most instructive analogue for an edge-deployed severity model.

### 8.1 Policy, the triage TPP, and the commercial landscape

WHO's 2021 guidance recommends CAD in place of human readers for digital CXR TB screening/triage in people ≥15 [WHO 2021], with CAD emitting a continuous abnormality score (typically 0–100) thresholded to trigger confirmatory molecular testing (Xpert MTB/RIF). The governing benchmark is the WHO Target Product Profile for a TB triage test: **≥90% sensitivity and ≥70% specificity**. Because abnormality-score distributions differ per vendor, there is *no universal threshold* [WHO/Stop TB Practical Guide 2021]. Four-to-five CE-marked products dominate: CAD4TB (Delft), qXR (Qure.ai), Lunit INSIGHT CXR, plus DrAid (VinBrain) and Genki (DeepTek).

### 8.2 Reported accuracy — and its collapse under realistic conditions

The most important finding for honest metric-reporting is that headline AUROCs of ~0.9 collapse toward 0.75–0.87 under realistic case mixes and microbiological truth:

- Qin et al. (Nepal+Cameroon, n=1,196, Xpert reference): AUROC Lunit 0.94, qXR 0.94, CAD4TB 0.92 — all matched or beat radiologists at equal sensitivity [Qin 2019]. This "lab-like" outpatient setting flatters CAD.
- Gelaw/Kik et al. (IOM migrant screening, 31 centres/18 countries, n=2,812): against a microbiological reference, AUROC dropped to CAD4TB 0.71, Lunit 0.85, qXR 0.75; against a radiological reference, ~0.81–0.87. **None met the WHO TPP** [Gelaw 2023].
- Worodria et al. / R2D2 TB Network (independent 7-country prospective, n=3,927): overall AUROC 0.774–0.819; at 90% sensitivity, specificity was CAD4TB 73.8%, qXR 70.3%, Lunit 70.0%, DrAid 67.9%, Genki 64.8%. With country-specific thresholds most products approached the TPP, but performance degraded for people living with HIV and prior-TB [Worodria 2024].

The consistent lesson: **local threshold calibration plus subgroup auditing are mandatory** before deployment.

### 8.3 Portable X-ray and offline LMIC deployment

The dominant real-world architecture pairs an ultra-portable digital X-ray with on-device CAD on a commodity laptop, fully offline. Alege et al. report Nigeria active case-finding: 94,694 screened, CAD4TB v7 at threshold 50, 10.84% presumptive yield, number-needed-to-screen 38, 50.6% same-day diagnosis, mean radiation 0.19 mSv, solar/off-grid operation [Alege 2025]. Two operationally decisive findings transfer directly: a fixed threshold *missed 47.6% of bacteriologically-confirmed cases* and was badly miscalibrated for children (only 2.1% of pediatric TB scored ≥50); and **off-grid power and sputum-transport logistics, not model FLOPs, were the binding constraints**. AI-based CXR triage is also cost-effective (~$39–40 per DALY averted, ~19% cost saving per 1,000 screened, false-positive treatment cost cut from 37% to 11%) [Tucker 2021].

### 8.4 The closest prior art and the severity target

The closest prior art to a frozen-ViT severity model is Kantipudi et al., who predict the **Timika score** (affected-lung-percentage [ALP] + 40 if cavity, range 0–140) on TB Portals/TBX11K: their best approach (DenseNet121 ALP regression + cavity classifier) achieved MAE 13.36–14.01% of range and Pearson 0.70–0.84 across country-held-out tests, with cavity AUC 0.80/0.88/0.85 [Kantipudi 2024]. This is the baseline to beat, and its country-held-out design validates Leave-One-Country-Out (LOCO) evaluation. The Timika score itself (ALP + 40 if ≥1 cavity, 0–140) is clinically validated for grading severity and predicting treatment outcome.

*(Verification correction: a "Reader variability and validation of the Timika X-ray score" citation previously attributed to Ralph et al. in Thorax is a three-way conflation — the title belongs to Thiel et al., IJTLD 2016, the original score is Ralph et al., Thorax 2010, and the cited URL pointed to a third, unrelated 2021 Indian Journal of Tuberculosis paper whose predictive AUC was actually 0.53–0.59. Only the score definition is safely reusable; the specific ">90% intra-reader agreement / AUC 0.68–0.75" claims should be dropped or re-sourced.)*

### 8.5 Efficient-ViT toolkit, uncertainty, and regulation for safe edge use

The efficient-ViT levers map onto TB deployment: training-free **ToMe** (2× throughput, 0.2–0.3% drop) [Bolya 2023], INT8 PTQ (~28% latency / ~70% memory on a Pixel), and ternary **BitMedViT** (16.88 ms on Jetson Orin Nano) [Walczak 2025]. For safe edge use, **conformal prediction** is the deployment-grade uncertainty method: "Conformal Triage" partitions patients into high-NPV / high-PPV / uncertain groups with statistical guarantees and defers uncertain cases to clinicians [Conformal Triage 2024]. On regulation, commercial CXR AI ships under CE and FDA 510(k); qXR holds 26+ FDA indications and is the only CADe device cleared with a Predetermined Change Control Plan, and is CE Class IIb cleared for pediatric (0–3y) TB [Qure.ai 2024]; the FDA distinguishes "locked" vs adaptive models under its AI-SaMD/GMLP/PCCP framework [FDA AI-SaMD], while **WHO Prequalification** is proposed as the LMIC-relevant pathway [Mateen 2025].

For the frozen RAD-DINO thread, this section both validates and sharpens the design: emitting a continuous Timika 0–140 severity is genuinely novel for edge/POC (all deployed CAD emits a binary-ish abnormality score), and should be positioned as a *post-triage severity layer* downstream of a WHO-TPP triage step. The kNN + transductive CORAL calibration directly answers the universal "scores don't transfer across countries" finding, label-free; the deep-ensemble + split-conformal layer instantiates the conformal-triage paradigm no deployed CAD currently offers. The deployment must report latency, post-quantization model size, memory, and *per-subgroup* (HIV+, pediatric, prior-TB) Timika error, since aggregate metrics hide the failures that matter clinically.

## 9. Federated / Privacy-Preserving Learning and System-Level Efficiency

Two interlocking threads close the survey: federated/privacy-preserving training across data-isolated sites, and system-level efficiency/measurement for no-GPU inference. The bridge — and the reason both matter for a frozen-encoder pipeline — is *distribution shift across countries* and the frozen-ViT compute bottleneck.

### 9.1 Federated foundations and non-IID heterogeneity

Federated learning was introduced by **FedAvg** [McMahan 2017], which keeps data on-device and averages locally-computed updates, cutting communication rounds 10–100× while tolerating non-IID data. The dominant medical failure mode is *client drift* under heterogeneity. **FedProx** [Li 2020] adds a proximal term to bound divergence. Crucially for imaging, much medical non-IID is *feature-shift* (different scanners, protocols, populations) rather than label-shift: **FedBN** [Li 2021] keeps BatchNorm statistics local while aggregating only conv/linear weights, outperforming FedAvg and FedProx on feature-shift benchmarks — the conceptual ancestor of cross-country (LOCO) personalization. **Babar et al.** empirically confirm measurable FedAvg degradation as medical-imaging heterogeneity rises [Babar 2024].

Real multi-country medical FL exists at scale: the **Federated Tumor Segmentation (FeTS)** effort benchmarked across 32 institutions in 17 countries (1,251 multimodal brain MRIs) [Zenk 2025]. *(Verification correction: lead author is Zenk et al.; it is 32 institutions, not 23.)* For the foundation-model era, **federated parameter-efficient fine-tuning (FedPEFT)** is the natural fit for a frozen encoder — only adapter weights are exchanged — but [Alkhunaizi 2024] quantifies the central trade-off: *every order-of-magnitude reduction in exchanged parameters costs ~4% accuracy* on out-of-domain/non-IID data, and medical-pretrained backbones beat general ones.

### 9.2 Privacy threat model and defences

FL is not private by default: gradient-inversion attacks reconstruct training images (AFGI reconstructs at batch sizes up to 48 [Liu 2024]). The dominant defences are **differential privacy** (DP-SGD with Gaussian mechanism + Rényi accounting) and **secure aggregation**. Imaging is comparatively DP-resilient: [Ziegler 2022] held DenseNet121 at AUC 0.94 at ε=6 on heterogeneous CXR across 36 clients, while ResNet50 collapsed to 0.76 — architecture choice dominates DP robustness. A 74-study review reports CXR AUC fell only 89.7→87.4% at ε=7.9 (but to 84.0% at ε=0.5), with pretraining essential, group-norm replacing batch-norm, and degradation amplified on small/heterogeneous data that can *widen subgroup fairness gaps* [Mohammadi 2026]. *(Verification corrections: first author is Marziyeh Mohammadi; CT classification "remained above 99% at ε=8" rather than a precise 99.3%.)*

### 9.3 On-device test-time adaptation and the federated CORAL analogue

**FedCTTA** [Rajib 2025] performs continual test-time adaptation across federated clients via entropy minimisation + similarity-aware aggregation over noise-probe outputs, avoiding feature exchange and holding constant memory — the privacy-clean federated analogue of transductive CORAL TTA. This means CORAL alignment can run purely on-device on frozen features, with no raw data leaving the clinic.

### 9.4 System-level efficiency, measurement, and compression interaction

Benchmarking methodology is codified by **MLPerf Mobile Inference** [Janapa Reddi 2022], with the measurement literature emphatic that **FLOPs is a poor proxy** for on-device latency/energy [Almeida 2021] (see §2). Quantization gives large communication savings in FL [Ren 2023] *(verification correction: the cited TLAQC paper achieves ~87–93% upload reduction, not "40–70%"; authors are Yaoyao Ren, Yu Cao et al.)*, but compression *interacts adversely* with federated updates — low-bit quantized/sparsified updates compound client drift, and DP noise on already-compressed updates degrades faster — so compression schedules must be co-designed with aggregation. **ONNX Runtime Web** offers a no-GPU, privacy-by-construction path (~20× over multi-threaded CPU on WebGPU) where DICOM can be decoded and processed client-side [Microsoft ORT-Web 2024]. At the extreme low-power end, batteryless/TinyML work targets energy-harvesting MCUs [Sabovic 2023] — bounding the spectrum, though an 87M-param ViT-B exceeds MCU budgets.

Medical-specific federated TB-CXR frameworks (hyper-network personalized FL; foundation-model-aware federated approaches) target cross-center heterogeneity with secure aggregation + DP. *(Verification note: a previously bundled "FHBench / multi-center TB FL" composite citation was malformed — FHBench is a general healthcare FL benchmark, not TB-specific — and its constituent works (HPFL, Multimedia Tools & Applications 2023; foundation-model federated work, World Wide Web 2024; FHBench, arXiv:2504.10817) should be cited individually rather than as one TB-specific entry.)*

For the frozen RAD-DINO thread, federation is cheap and natural: a privacy-preserving collaborative TB Portals deployment is essentially *federated PEFT* exchanging only tiny head weights, with the FedBN lesson translating to keeping site-local normalization/calibration (per-country affine, locally-anchored kNN) while sharing head weights under FedProx-style regularization. Moderate-ε DP (≈6–10) keeps CXR AUC within ~2–3 points; the open validation is whether ALP *regression* and conformal *coverage* degrade gracefully under DP noise and federation — an interaction the literature does not characterise.

## 10. Open Problems & Research Gaps

Synthesising across all seven threads, the following gaps are both field-wide and specifically load-bearing for a frozen-ViT medical severity model on edge:

1. **Sub-INT8 PTQ of self-supervised foundation backbones.** Nearly all ViT-PTQ numbers (PTQ4ViT, RepQ-ViT) are on *supervised* ImageNet ViT/DeiT/Swin. Whether RepQ-style or AdaLog-style fixes hold for a *self-distilled DINOv2/RAD-DINO* encoder (no classification head, register tokens, different activation statistics) is essentially unmeasured.

2. **Compression vs the attention-map explanation.** Across quantization (FQ-ViT log-domain Softmax demonstrably alters attention sharpness), token merging (mixes spatial provenance), and distillation (only qualitatively verified, e.g. [Mir 2025]), there is *no standard metric* — attention-map IoU, rank correlation, pointing-game vs radiologist masks — certifying that a compressed model's explanation survives. For a system whose attention map *is* the cavity explanation, "explanation fidelity vs compression budget" is an acute, unmeasured trade-off that the project can turn into a contribution.

3. **Regression and ordinal severity under compression.** The medical compression literature is dominated by classification/segmentation. The sensitivity of a *continuous* target (Timika 0–140, with no softmax to absorb error) to quantization noise, token reduction, distillation, and DP noise — and its interaction with imbalance-aware losses like Balanced-MSE — is largely unstudied.

4. **Uncertainty/calibration under compression, federation, and shift.** No work connects low-bit inference, token reduction, distillation, or DP noise to *split-conformal coverage validity* or deep-ensemble diversity. Worse, conformal guarantees assume exchangeability that LOCO/cross-country shift breaks; federated and shift-robust conformal calibration is open, and whether transductive CORAL adaptation leaks patient information is uncharacterised.

5. **Preserving the patch-token grid through distillation.** Most SSL-distillation losses match global embeddings; dense per-patch distillation that keeps the 37×37→7×7 spatial structure intact for attention pooling is rarely benchmarked, and naive feature-map MSE fails on low-rank ViT features [Tian 2025].

6. **Honest cross-backend edge benchmarking for medical ViTs.** No standardised benchmark reports DINOv2/RAD-DINO-class encoder latency, energy, *and peak memory* across Core ML/ANE, TFLite-NNAPI/QNN, ORT-Web/WebGPU/WASM, and commodity CPU simultaneously, with thermal-state and numeric-parity disclosure. Existing studies are iPhone-only or generic-ViT; the exact 518×518/1369-token regime is unmeasured, and ANE-friendly rewriting of arbitrary HF ViT attention remains manual.

7. **Dynamic-token-count export gap.** Adaptive methods (DynamicViT, A-ViT, AdaViT) emit variable token counts that frequently *cannot* compile to static-graph mobile runtimes — an under-reported obstacle that favours fixed-ratio merging for edge.

8. **Stacked-compression Pareto frontier.** Few works jointly tune token reduction + low-bit quantization + (structured) pruning + distillation under a single edge latency/memory budget; the Pareto frontier for stacked compression on medical ViTs is uncharted, as is its adverse interaction with federation and DP.

9. **Deployment-grade severity scoring and threshold portability.** No deployed CAD outputs a continuous severity/Timika score; every TB study concludes vendor scores need per-country recalibration, yet there is no automated, label-free, field-validated recalibration method — a direct opening for transductive CORAL + conformal recalibration. Subgroup fragility (HIV+, pediatric, prior-TB) is documented and persistent, and pediatric/HIV+ *severity* is almost wholly unstudied at the edge.

10. **Regulatory status of test-time-adapted models.** Whether transductive CORAL alignment at inference constitutes a "model change" under an FDA Predetermined Change Control Plan or WHO Prequalification — i.e. the locked-vs-adaptive distinction — is unresolved for an LMIC severity tool.
