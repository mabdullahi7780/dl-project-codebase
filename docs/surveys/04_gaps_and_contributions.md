# Research Gaps & Candidate Paper Contributions

## Identified Research Gaps


### Gap 1: Explanation fidelity under compression is never measured: ViT compression papers report task accuracy but never whether the attention/saliency map survives quantization, token reduction, or distillation. For a system whose attention map IS the cavity explanation, this is an acute, ownable white space.

- **Evidence it's real / under-explored:** Survey C WS1 + Part C1.1: every ViT-PTQ paper (PTQ4ViT [Yuan 2022], RepQ-ViT [Li 2023a], FQ-ViT [Lin 2022]) reports only top-1 accuracy after quantization; 'none reports whether the attention/saliency map is preserved.' FQ-ViT's Log-Int-Softmax 'demonstrably alters attention sharpness' by requantizing the softmax the head pools over; RepQ-ViT reparameterizes the LayerNorm/Softmax quantizers — the two ops that shape an attention map. Survey B Open Problem 2 states there is 'no standard metric' (attention-map IoU, rank correlation, pointing-game vs radiologist masks) certifying a compressed model's explanation survives. The one medical KD study checking explanation survival (Mir 2025, Score-CAM) does so 'only qualitatively.' Survey A general fragility results (Ghorbani 2019; Heo 2019) show saliency drifts under accuracy-preserving weight/numeric manipulation — and compression IS exactly that, so the a priori expectation is drift.
- **Why publishable:** It is a named intersection gap that neither the XAI nor the edge literature owns (Survey B OP2 = Survey A OP6). The team can produce the first explanation-fidelity-vs-compression-budget curve, and the experiment is training-free on cached features (PTQ + ToMe), so it fits free-tier T4. Even a null result (INT8 preserves the map) is a useful first data point; a positive result (map drifts before accuracy does) is a clean, novel finding.

### Gap 2: Split-conformal coverage validity under quantization is unstudied — in particular whether the FP-calibrate-then-quantize deployment pattern silently breaks the coverage guarantee, and how INT8/INT4 noise interacts with already-fragile cross-country (LOCO) coverage.

- **Evidence it's real / under-explored:** Survey C WS2 + Part C1.3, quoting Survey B Open Problem 4 verbatim: 'no work connects low-bit inference, token reduction, distillation, or DP noise to split-conformal coverage validity or deep-ensemble diversity.' Survey C reasons that quantization changes the nonconformity score function: calibrate-after-quantize should hold by construction, but FP-calibrate-then-deploy-quantized makes calibration scores mismatch deployed scores and 'coverage can break' — 'this FP-calibrate-then-quantize failure mode is not characterised anywhere in either survey.' Conformal exchangeability is broken by LOCO shift (Survey A §7.3; Tibshirani 2019; Barber 2023), and the team's Moldova fold already under-covers at 0.83 vs nominal 0.90 — 'exactly the kind of margin that low-bit noise could erode further.'
- **Why publishable:** The intersection of UQ and compression is, per the survey, essentially empty. The team owns the exact assets (split-conformal layer, M=10 ensemble, documented Moldova under-coverage, LOCO protocol) to run the experiment, which is post-hoc and free-tier-T4-feasible. The calibrate-order ablation is a crisp, reproducible methodological result with direct deployment-safety stakes.

### Gap 3: The faithfulness of learned attention-pooling weights on FROZEN self-supervised features is essentially untested — the entire faithfulness literature targets backbone-level maps (rollout, Chefer, AttnLRP), not a trained downstream attention-pool head on frozen DINOv2/RAD-DINO features.

- **Evidence it's real / under-explored:** Survey A Open Problem 1: 'Almost nobody has rigorously tested whether the softmax weights of a trained downstream attention-pool head on frozen features are faithful — exactly the project archetype's setting.' Survey C WS3 restates this and notes the team's head 'is exactly attention-based MIL [Ilse 2018] on frozen features.' Survey A §4.5 warns attention-pool weights are 'correlational, not causal' and inherit every faithfulness caveat. Survey A §6.5: TB transformer-XAI work 'overwhelmingly defaults to Grad-CAM heatmaps... and rarely applies faithfulness tests.' Caron 2021/Darcet 2024 add that self-supervised attention segments the salient object (and may carry high-norm artifact tokens), not necessarily the target pathology — so the map cannot be assumed disease-specific without validation.
- **Why publishable:** The team has the rare combination of (a) the exact untested architecture, (b) TB Portals sextant-level cavity annotations as ground truth (Known Limitation i is precisely that this validation is missing), and (c) cached frozen features making the whole battery (EBPG, MPRT, token-deletion) nearly free. Converts a qualitative figure into a validated localizer; an honest negative (fails MPRT, or under-localises small cavities as CheXlocalize found) is itself publishable.

### Gap 4: Sub-INT8 post-training quantization recipes are validated only on supervised ImageNet ViT/DeiT/Swin; whether they transfer to a self-distilled, classification-head-free, register-token-free medical DINOv2 encoder (RAD-DINO) is unmeasured.

- **Evidence it's real / under-explored:** Survey B Open Problem 1: 'Nearly all ViT-PTQ numbers (PTQ4ViT, RepQ-ViT) are on supervised ImageNet ViT/DeiT/Swin. Whether RepQ-style or AdaLog-style fixes hold for a self-distilled DINOv2/RAD-DINO encoder (no classification head, register tokens, different activation statistics) is essentially unmeasured.' Survey C WS4 reiterates. Survey B §3.5/§3.2 documents the three transformer PTQ pathologies (post-LayerNorm variance, power-law post-Softmax, asymmetric post-GELU) and warns INT8 PTQ for ViTs is fragile (OpenVINO/NNCF) and can even regress latency on some mobile cores (Z. Li 2025, QNNPACK GELU 2.85x).
- **Why publishable:** RAD-DINO's activation statistics differ from supervised ViTs (no supervised classification objective, image-only DINOv2 self-distillation), so the open question is genuinely unsettled and directly answerable. The team has the encoder and cached pipeline; PTQ is calibration-only (32 in-domain CXRs, minutes). Pairs naturally with the explanation-fidelity and conformal-coverage measurements to make one substantive deployment paper.

### Gap 5: Explaining and conformalising a COMPOSITE clinical score that mixes a continuous regression term and a thresholded binary term (Timika = ALP + 40·1[cavity], range 0-140) is largely untouched; almost all attribution and conformal machinery targets classification logits or a single continuous output.

- **Evidence it's real / under-explored:** Survey A Open Problem 5 ('Explaining regression and composite clinical scores... a composite score mixing a regression term and a thresholded binary term, is under-explored — including which target to backprop and how to compose heterogeneous explanations') and Open Problem 9 ('Conformal inference for a composite score combining continuous regression and thresholded binary terms, while preserving clinically meaningful interval semantics, is largely untouched'). Survey B Open Problem 3: the medical compression literature is 'dominated by classification/segmentation'; a continuous target 'with no softmax to absorb error' under compression is unstudied, including its interaction with Balanced-MSE. Survey C WS5 unifies these and ties them to the team's known calibration-slope<1 (0.55-0.68) severe-band under-prediction.
- **Why publishable:** Timika is exactly the composite regression+threshold target the literature flags as untouched, and the team already computes it with CQR-amenable continuous ALP plus a binary cavity flag. The contribution (separate conformal handling of the two terms, composed into a coherent interval; fixing the Moldova under-coverage and the slope<1 severe-band failure) is post-hoc, free-tier-T4, and reusable by any composite-score system.

### Gap 6: The interaction between model compression and cross-country (LOCO) generalization + calibration is uncharacterised: no work measures whether quantization/token-reduction/distillation erode the cross-domain robustness gap or break conformal coverage under simultaneous covariate and label shift.

- **Evidence it's real / under-explored:** Survey C Part C1.3: compression is a 'static shift in the model itself,' LOCO is a 'data shift,' DP noise a third, and 'their interaction with a quantized score function is unstudied'; conformal guarantees 'assume exchangeability that LOCO/cross-country shift breaks' (Survey A §7.3; Tibshirani 2019; Barber 2023). Survey B Open Problem 4 lists shift-robust conformal calibration under federation/shift as open. Survey B §8.2 provides the deployment evidence that vendor TB-CAR scores collapse and need per-country recalibration (Gelaw 2023 AUROC dropped to 0.71-0.85; Worodria 2024 7-country 0.774-0.819) — i.e. cross-country robustness is the fragile axis in practice. The project memory makes 'compression must not erode the LOCO gap or conformal coverage' a non-negotiable design constraint.
- **Why publishable:** This reframes the team's headline ICONIP novelty (the cross-country gap decomposition) as a first-class compression-evaluation axis that nobody else measures. It is a defensible, asset-specific contribution: the team has the LOCO protocol, three-country folds, kNN+transductive CORAL alignment, and conformal layer — exactly the apparatus to ask whether an on-device model keeps the cross-country win. The result is meaningful either way (gap preserved = strong deployment story; gap collapses = an important honest warning).

## Recommended Contributions


### C1: Validating and cheaply upgrading attention-pool explanations on frozen self-supervised CXR features (the R4b localizer paper)

- **Idea:** Convert the qualitative spatial cavity-attention overlay into a faithfulness-validated, ground-truth-localized cavity explainer, and add a near-zero-cost class-specific upgrade. Directly addresses Survey A OP1 / Survey C WS3 (faithfulness of attention-pool weights on frozen features) and Known Limitation (i). Frame the head explicitly as attention-based MIL [Ilse 2018] in Hou et al.'s self-explainable attention branch, claim faithfulness (Jacovi-Goldberg), not just upper-lobe concentration.
- **Experiment:** Primary localization metric: Energy-Based Pointing Game (EBPG, fraction of attention energy inside the GT sextant) against TB Portals sextant cavity annotations, with Pointing Game and an IoU/Dice threshold sweep as complements. Faithfulness floor: MPRT (randomize head weights, show the map degrades) — cheap because the head is trivial. Token-level deletion/insertion with ROAD-style neighbour-mean imputation (not zeroing) ablating top-k attended tokens and measuring Timika logit drop; only the head re-evaluates while the frozen ViT forward is cached. Then upgrade raw softmax attention to a CDAM/Chefer signed, class-specific map (+1 backward through the head) and show a faithfulness gain (EBPG, deletion-AUC, SaCo). Baselines: raw attention, attention rollout, Grad-CAM on the patch grid (the TB-field default), random attribution. Orchestrate in Quantus. Also inspect patch-token norms for Darcet-style artifact tokens before pooling.
- **Feasibility:** High. Trivial compute on cached frozen features; runs in hours on free-tier T4. The only hard dependency is access to TB Portals sextant-level cavity annotations (an existing project asset). On a frozen backbone, gradients into the encoder are uninformative, so CDAM may add little over raw attention — that null is itself a clean result and motivates the deployment paper.
- **Target venue:** ISBI or MICCAI (interpretable-CXR) / MICCAI iMIMIC workshop; strong immediate ICONIP follow-up.

### C2: Does the explanation survive compression? An explanation-fidelity-vs-compression-budget benchmark for a frozen-ViT CXR severity model

- **Idea:** Produce the first quantitative certificate that a compressed model's attention/cavity explanation survives, as a function of the compression budget. Addresses Survey C WS1 (Survey B OP2 = Survey A OP6) and the sub-INT8-PTQ-of-RAD-DINO gap (WS4 / Survey B OP1). The central, ownable insight: ViT compression papers report only task accuracy and never explanation survival, yet the team's attention map IS the clinical explanation.
- **Experiment:** Sweep three compression levers on the frozen encoder: (a) INT8/INT4 PTQ via RepQ-ViT/SmoothQuant with 32 in-domain CXR calibration images (heads kept FP); (b) token-keep-rate via training-free ToMe/ATM with proportional-attention bookkeeping; (c) distilled-student size (shared with the distillation paper). For each operating point report attention-map IoU / Spearman rank correlation / EBPG between the FP and compressed model, alongside Timika MAE and measured latency/peak-memory. This yields explanation-fidelity-vs-bit-width and EBPG-vs-keep-rate curves — the publishable white space. Run MPRT before and after each compression step. Compare naive zero-baseline ablation vs ROAD imputation to control the OOD confound. Report whether RepQ-style fixes hold on the self-distilled RAD-DINO encoder (no classification head, different activation stats).
- **Feasibility:** High. PTQ is calibration-only (minutes) and ToMe is training-free; everything runs on cached features on free-tier T4. The distilled-student rows depend on the distillation contribution but the PTQ + token-merge rows stand alone, so the paper is viable even without A100. Needs the P0.1 metric harness from the localizer paper.
- **Target venue:** MIDL or IEEE JBHI for the full benchmark; a NeurIPS/ICCV XAI or efficient-ML workshop for an early cut.

### C3: Conformal coverage under quantization for a composite TB severity score under cross-country shift

- **Idea:** Characterise whether split-conformal coverage survives INT8/INT4 quantization, expose the FP-calibrate-then-quantize failure mode, and deliver a conformal treatment of the composite Timika = ALP + 40·1[cavity] target that fixes the team's Moldova under-coverage (0.83) and calibration-slope<1 severe-band under-prediction. Addresses Survey C WS2+WS5, Survey B OP4/OP3, Survey A OP9.
- **Experiment:** Conformalize the continuous ALP term with CQR and the thresholded cavity term via a separate calibrated/conformal classifier, composed into a coherent interval on Timika. Pair split-CP with weighted / non-exchangeable CP (Tibshirani 2019; Barber 2023) for LOCO shift and report Mondrian per-country coverage. Core ablation: empirical coverage at alpha=0.10 and interval width for FP vs INT8 vs INT4, and crucially calibrate-then-quantize vs quantize-then-calibrate, to show whether the deployment-standard FP-calibration silently breaks coverage. Exploit the head-only ensemble structure (run the frozen ViT once, ensemble M=10 cheap heads) so the UQ is near-free on edge. Baselines: constant-width split-CP, FP-only coverage, temperature scaling. Metrics: marginal + per-country coverage, interval width, calibration slope.
- **Feasibility:** High. Entirely post-hoc on cached features and the existing conformal/ensemble ladder; free-tier T4. Risk: the binary cavity term may resist clean single-interval conformalisation, forcing an interval + flag output — which is still a defensible, reportable design.
- **Target venue:** ML4H or IEEE JBHI; strong fit for a NeurIPS/ICML safe-ML / UQ workshop.

### C4: Distilling a frozen CXR foundation encoder to an edge student without eroding the cross-country gap or the cavity explanation

- **Idea:** Distill the frozen RAD-DINO ViT-B into a small token-grid student that runs at MobileNet-speed on edge, while preserving BOTH the LOCO cross-country generalization gap (the team's headline novelty, per the non-negotiable memory constraint) AND the cavity-attention explanation. Addresses the compression x LOCO-robustness gap and the patch-token-grid-preservation gap (Survey B OP5; Survey C C1.1). The reframing nobody else does: treat cross-country robustness preservation as a first-class distillation objective.
- **Experiment:** Re-run the DINOv2 iBOT objective with frozen RAD-DINO as teacher into an EfficientFormerV2-S0 / TinyViT / ViT-S-class token-producing student; add SEED similarity-distribution or DisCo embedding matching, per-patch feature matching with feature-lifting to handle low-rank ViT features (Tian 2025), and an explicit attention-matching KD term (Zagoruyko 2017; A. Li 2024) to preserve the cavity map. Distill on TB Portals + a public CXR pool. Re-fit the calibration ladder (kNN, CORAL, conformal) on student features and re-validate. Report, with pre-registered tolerances: Timika MAE per country, the LOCO MAE gap vs teacher, EBPG preservation vs sextant annotations, conformal coverage, and on-device latency after Core ML INT8 export. Baselines: from-scratch student, embedding-only KD (no attention term), frozen-teacher linear probe, and the published TB baselines the paper already beats (TXV+CheXzero, GroupDRO, IW-regression).
- **Feasibility:** Medium — the one contribution that genuinely benefits from an A100. SSL distillation over a large CXR pool is the heavy step; TinyViT's teacher-logit-caching trick makes a T4 run tractable but slow, so prototype on T4 with cached teacher features and scale on A100 if obtainable. Flagged risks (attention KD insufficient to localise cavities; student loses RAD-DINO's ~880k-CXR cross-country robustness) are both publishable negatives.
- **Target venue:** MICCAI or IEEE JBHI (clinical/deployment framing); efficient-ML workshop for a methods-only cut. Natural flagship for the MICCAI-2027-aligned thread.

### C5: A four-backend on-device Pareto frontier (faithfulness x latency x peak memory) for a RAD-DINO-class CXR severity encoder

- **Idea:** Build the missing benchmark: jointly measure explanation faithfulness, latency, energy, and peak memory for the 518x518 / 1369-token RAD-DINO regime across iPad (Core ML/ANE), Android (TFLite-QNN/Hexagon), browser (ORT-Web/WebGPU/WASM), and commodity CPU. Addresses Survey B OP6 and Survey A OP6 — no such benchmark exists for this exact regime. Distinctive systems-plus-clinical framing.
- **Experiment:** Export the FP, INT8, token-merged, and distilled encoders to all four targets; for iPad apply the mandatory ANE attention rewrite ((B,C,1,S) layout, Conv2d-1x1 for Linear, reshape-free attention) since a stock export silently lands on GPU/CPU. Measure under MLPerf-Mobile methodology with thermal-state and numeric-parity disclosure, enforcing the FLOPs-not-equal-latency caveat (including the documented INT8-can-regress-latency-on-some-cores pathology). Plot faithfulness (EBPG drift vs FP) against measured latency and peak memory, and report per-subgroup (HIV+, pediatric, prior-TB) Timika error since aggregate metrics hide the failures that matter clinically. Keep the heads in FP and ensemble only the heads on the shared backbone forward.
- **Feasibility:** Medium. Inference-only — no training — so GPU compute is NOT the constraint; the binding constraint is physical device access (an iPad, a Snapdragon tablet, a browser, a mini-PC). Some backends may lack coverage for specific attention ops, which is itself a reportable finding. Depends on the distilled student (small-model rows) and the INT8 encoder.
- **Target venue:** IEEE JBHI or a systems/efficient-ML venue (MLSys or NeurIPS efficient-ML workshop); the combined systems + clinical-subgroup framing is the differentiator.

## Completeness Critic


**Overall:** All three surveys are strong, well-structured, and unusually disciplined about verification — the explicit REFUTED/overstatement/conflation flagging is a real strength and most corrections (IG uniqueness, RISE mask counts, jackknife+ URL, Folio author removal, FDA PCCP document split) check out against primary sources. The integration survey (C) is the most valuable artifact: its white-space identification (WS1–WS5) and P0/P1/P2 roadmap are concrete, compute-aware, and correctly anchored to the project's actual assets. However, three things should be fixed before submission. (1) FACTUAL: the RAD-DINO corpus size is wrong/inconsistent — the verified figure is 838,336 (~838k); Survey B's ~883k and C's inherited number overstate it, and Survey A's correct ~840k should be propagated to all three. (2) FACTUAL: the DINO 'both verified exactly' (78.3% kNN is ViT-S/8, not ViT-B) and the DeiT '83.0 vs 81.8 = hard vs soft' (it's hard vs no-distillation) are real errors. (3) CONSISTENCY: the headline 'beats baselines by 8.5 Timika points' is not reconciled with the Kantipudi baseline once its '13–14% of range' is unit-converted to ~19 Timika points — the named baselines (TXV+CheXzero, GroupDRO, IW-regression) are never quantified in any survey, so the central competitive claim is unsupported by the literature review. The biggest COMPLETENESS gaps are: counterfactual/generative explanations (absent from A despite being natural for a regression target), explanation-evaluation metrics adapted to a continuous/composite target (flagged as open but never methodologically developed), risk-controlling prediction sets / Learn-then-Test (missing from the UQ section), and the CORAL-TTA × quantization interaction (more load-bearing for this exact system than the federated thread that gets a full roadmap item). The weakest-vs-importance mismatch is Survey A §6.5 (CXR attention faithfulness): only two citations for the single most load-bearing sub-literature, which is also precisely the project's core contribution — it should be expanded or explicitly reframed as the gap being filled. Net: publishable foundation, but fix the ~838k number everywhere, correct the DINO/DeiT number attributions, and quantify-or-drop the 8.5-point baseline claim."}


**Missing topics:**
- SURVEY A (XAI) — Counterfactual / contrastive explanations are entirely absent. For a regression severity score (Timika 0–140), 'what minimal change to the lung field would lower the predicted ALP/cavity probability' is a natural and clinically intuitive explanation modality (counterfactual/diffusion-based CXR editing, e.g. latent-shift, StylEx-class generative counterfactuals). A reviewer of an XAI survey aimed at a severity model would expect at least a paragraph.
- SURVEY A — ReciproCAM / Recipro-CAM and other gradient-free-but-cheap CAM variants are name-dropped once in the open-problems list ('Chefer/rollout/AttnLRP/ReciproCAM disagree') but never introduced or cited in the body. Either introduce it in §3.2/§4 or drop the dangling reference.
- SURVEY A — No treatment of explanation evaluation for REGRESSION/continuous targets specifically. EBPG, Pointing Game, deletion/insertion, comprehensiveness/sufficiency are all defined for classification logits. The survey flags 'explaining regression' as an open problem (OP5) but never discusses how the faithfulness metrics themselves must be adapted (which scalar to perturb-and-measure, signed vs unsigned attribution for a continuous output). This is the project's actual setting and deserves a subsection, not just an open-problem bullet.
- SURVEY A — Spatial/Mondrian conformal and risk-controlling prediction sets (RCPS, Bates et al. 2021; Learn-then-Test, Angelopoulos et al. 2021) are missing from §7.3. For an imaging severity task where you may want guarantees on a derived risk (e.g. false-negative cavity rate), RCPS/LTT is the natural tool and is a glaring omission next to CQR/jackknife+/weighted-CP.
- SURVEY B (Edge) — Activation/KV memory and peak-RAM analysis of the 1369-token ViT-B forward is never quantified, even though peak memory is repeatedly named the binding constraint for browser/low-RAM tablets. The survey should at least back-of-envelope the attention activation footprint (1369² attention matrix × heads × layers) that motivates token reduction, since that is the actual reason ToMe matters more than weight quantization on a memory-bound target.
- SURVEY B — FlashAttention / memory-efficient attention and operator-level kernel fusion are absent. On WebGPU and mobile NPUs the attention kernel implementation (fused vs materialized N×N) dominates the memory-bound regime the survey keeps invoking; omitting it leaves the 'FLOPs≠latency' thesis under-supported at the kernel level.
- SURVEY B — Caching strategy for the frozen encoder at INFERENCE time on edge is not discussed, only at training time. A real LMIC clinic re-scans patients; whether/how to cache or reuse encoder features across the calibration ladder on-device (and the security of an on-device feature cache) is a concrete deployment question left untouched.
- SURVEY B — Thermal throttling and sustained-throughput (vs single-shot latency) is mentioned in §2 as a caveat but never operationalized; for a clinic doing batch screening of dozens of CXRs, sustained ms/image under thermal load is the metric that matters, and no source on sustained-load mobile inference is cited.
- SURVEY C (Integration) — No discussion of the order-of-operations / composition between CORAL test-time feature alignment and the compressed encoder. CORAL shifts features at test time; if the encoder is quantized, CORAL statistics are computed on quantized features — the interaction of TTA with quantization is arguably a sixth white-space item (it sits between WS2 and WS4) and is more directly load-bearing for THIS system than the federated item that is given a full P2.3.
- SURVEY C — The composite-score conformal contribution (P2.1 / Contribution 3) never engages with the fact that Timika = ALP + 40·1[cavity] is a SUM of a continuous and a Bernoulli term, so its conformal interval is a convolution/mixture, not a simple interval. The survey gestures at 'two-number output' as a fallback but does not cite any work on conformal prediction for sums of heterogeneous predictors or for monotone transformations of multiple conformalized quantities — a literature gap that should be named as part of WS5 rather than assumed tractable.
- SURVEY C — Energy/battery as a first-class axis is missing from the four-backend Pareto frontier (Contribution 5). For genuinely off-grid/solar LMIC clinics (which Survey B's Alege 2025 explicitly flags as power-constrained), energy-per-inference is arguably more decision-relevant than latency, yet C5 lists 'faithfulness × latency × memory' and only mentions energy in passing.

**Weak sections:**
- SURVEY A §2 Taxonomy — The DINO emergent-attention numbers are stated as 'both verified exactly' but conflate two architectures: per the source (Caron 2021), 78.3% kNN is ViT-S/8, while 80.1% linear is ViT-B. Presenting both as a single ViT-B result is wrong and undermines the 'verified' framing. Also these are DINO (v1) numbers being used to justify a DINOv2/RAD-DINO encoder — the architectural lineage should be stated, not implied.
- SURVEY A §5.1 — CXR-NeXus (Cureus) is the ONLY cited TB-specific prototype method and is correctly flagged as a low-bar venue, but the section then leans on it as the 'closest interpretable-CXR related work' in the relevance box. Leaning a related-work claim on a single-author pay-to-publish paper is thin; the section needs either a stronger TB/CXR ante-hoc citation or an explicit acknowledgement that no strong TB ante-hoc prior art exists.
- SURVEY A §6.5 — 'Attention faithfulness in CXR specifically' is genuinely thin (two citations, Wollek and Chung), and the survey admits it. Given this is the single most load-bearing sub-literature for the project (the model's explanation IS attention), this is the weakest-vs-importance mismatch in the whole survey and should be expanded or explicitly framed as the core gap the project fills.
- SURVEY B §5.4 / §8.4 — The Kantipudi 'closest prior art' baseline (the actual number to beat) is given DenseNet121 MAE 13.36–14.01% of range, but the project's headline results are reported in raw Timika points (~16.7–21.6). The two surveys never reconcile the units: 13–14% of a 0–140 range ≈ 18–20 Timika points, which is roughly the SAME ballpark as the project's claimed result — so the 'beats baselines by up to 8.5 Timika points' claim is NOT clearly supported against Kantipudi and the surveys should make the unit conversion explicit rather than leaving a reviewer to suspect the baseline is misquoted or the win overstated.
- SURVEY B §5.2 — The DeiT distillation framing ('hard distillation 83.0% vs soft KL 81.8%') is misleading: per the DeiT paper, 81.8% is the NO-distillation DeiT-B baseline and 83.0% is hard distillation; soft distillation is a separate (higher, ~81.x at the comparable setting) number. The 'hard beats soft' claim is true in the paper but the specific number pairing cited here looks like hard-vs-no-distillation mislabeled as hard-vs-soft. Re-source the exact ablation row.
- SURVEY B §3.4 — The medical quantization evidence leans heavily on a single 2026 MDPI Technologies review whose author list the survey itself admits 'was not surfaced.' Building the headline 'INT8 is the workhorse, <1–3% loss' claim on an un-author-verified review is weak; pair it with the (verified) Abid 2021 CXR-specific numbers as the primary evidence and demote the review to corroboration.
- SURVEY C Part C2 table — Several rows assert quantitative latency/memory wins ('~28% latency / ~70% memory on a Pixel', '~10× faster / ~14× lower peak memory') by importing figures from Survey B that are themselves caveated as regime-specific (e.g. the Apple 10×/14× holds only at long sequence/batch, with the seq-128/batch-1 figure being 2.84×). Re-importing the optimistic end of a caveated range into a 'targets/impact' table without the caveat overstates the achievable wins for the project's batch-1, fixed-518² regime.
- SURVEY C C1.3 — The central claim 'if the model is calibrated AFTER quantization on a held-out INT8 calibration set, split-CP coverage should hold by construction' is stated with more confidence than warranted. Split-CP coverage holds under EXCHANGEABILITY of calibration and test points through the SAME (quantized) score function — which is fine — but the survey then immediately invokes LOCO cross-country shift, which already breaks exchangeability regardless of quantization. The interaction is muddled: the quantization point and the shift point are conflated, and the 'holds by construction' assurance is only true in-distribution.
- SURVEY C P1.1 / Contribution 4 — The distillation plan to preserve the LOCO gap is the flagship contribution but rests on an unexamined tension: RAD-DINO's cross-country robustness plausibly comes from its 838k-CXR pretraining scale, and a student distilled only on TB Portals + a modest public pool may not inherit it. The survey acknowledges this as a 'risk' but does not propose how to MEASURE whether the generalization is coming from the teacher's features vs the distillation data, leaving the headline constraint (preserve LOCO gap) without a concrete falsification protocol.

**Factual concerns:**
- RAD-DINO training-set size is inconsistent across the three surveys and the project brief, and one value is wrong. The authoritative figure (Pérez-García 2024 / arXiv:2401.10815, the Multi-CXR corpus) is 838,336 images ≈ 838k. Survey A's '~840k (the paper's own figure)' is CORRECT. Survey B states '~883k CXRs from five public datasets (MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert, NIH-CXR, PadChest, BRAX)' — this is OVERSTATED and the 883k figure is not the paper's headline number (838k).
