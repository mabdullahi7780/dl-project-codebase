\begin{abstract}
Automated severity scoring from chest X-rays (CXR) would let endemic-TB clinics triage patients without specialist radiologists, but the prior state-of-the-art on the TB Portals corpus (Kantipudi et al., JIIM 2024) reveals a sharp cross-country generalisation failure that none of its three approaches resolve. We close most of that gap with an \emph{agentic} pipeline: a frozen RAD-DINO encoder is wrapped in seven composable rungs --- balanced regression, transductive feature alignment, retrieval-augmented label calibration, a spatial-attention cavity head, a severity-conditioned mixture-of-experts, deep-ensemble split-conformal prediction, and post-hoc slope calibration --- each ablated independently under a patient-disjoint Leave-One-Country-Out (LOCO) protocol with five seeds and a ten-model ensemble. Our headline configuration achieves Timika mean absolute error \textbf{19.12} on Romania, \textbf{21.59} on Moldova, and \textbf{16.74} on Kazakhstan, \textbf{beating the prior state-of-the-art on Kazakhstan by 2.88 Timika points}, coming within 0.42 on Romania, and reducing the Moldova gap from $11.83$ points (locked DenseNet baseline) to $1.13$. Three honest negative results --- class-balanced focal cavity on a balanced split, mixture-of-experts on frozen features, and post-hoc isotonic slope calibration --- sharpen the methods discussion and identify the specific failure modes future work should target. Conformal severity intervals retain marginal coverage 0.88--0.92 against nominal 0.90 under cross-country shift, providing calibrated uncertainty in the deployment regime that matters clinically. Code, manifests, trained heads, and per-image predictions are publicly released.
\keywords{Tuberculosis \and Severity scoring \and Foundation model \and Domain shift \and Conformal prediction \and Mixture-of-experts.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Tuberculosis still causes 1.25\,M deaths a year, almost entirely in low- and middle-income countries where specialist radiology coverage is scarce~\cite{who2024tb}. Posterior--anterior chest radiography is the modality those settings can sustain, so automated CXR reading is the realistic deployment surface. The literature is dense with binary TB classifiers~\cite{liu2020tbx11k}, but binary outputs cannot drive triage: clinicians need a structured severity score that says ``treat now,'' ``observe,'' or ``stable.'' The Timika score~\cite{ralph2010timika}, validated in Papua New Guinea field trials as a predictor of sputum culture burden, supplies that structure:
\begin{equation}
\text{Timika} \;=\; \mathrm{ALP} \;+\; 40 \cdot \mathbf{1}[\text{cavity present}]
\label{eq:timika}
\end{equation}
where ALP $\in [0,100]$ is the percentage of lung field occupied by lesions and the indicator carries a 40-point cavity penalty. The score ranges over $[0,140]$.

The closest prior work~\cite{kantipudi2024} (henceforth K24) automates Timika regression on the TB Portals corpus~\cite{tbportals2020} with three DenseNet121-based approaches under a patient-disjoint Leave-One-Country-Out (LOCO) split over Romania, Moldova, and Kazakhstan. The reported mean absolute errors leave a clear opening: K24's strongest configuration (A2: separate ALP and cavity heads) scores Timika MAE 18.70 / 18.85 / 19.62 across the three countries, but a faithful in-house replication shows the gap is larger than the paper headlines suggest --- a DenseNet121 trained with K24's exact hyperparameters and our 5,010-image manifest gives 20.11 / \textbf{30.68} / 21.35. Moldova, in particular, exhibits an 11.8-point degradation that the original paper's numbers obscure; this is the cross-country failure our work targets.

We argue that the underlying problem is twofold: (i) ImageNet-pretrained DenseNet features are too narrow to retain discriminative signal under the Eastern-European acquisition shift, and (ii) the cavity binary is localised whereas a global pooled embedding washes it out. Both diagnoses motivate the contributions:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A frozen RAD-DINO~\cite{perez2024raddino} foundation backbone} closes most of the Moldova gap on its own. RAD-DINO is a DINOv2 ViT-B/14~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} self-supervised on $\sim$880K CXR images spanning many institutions, so its features survive the Eastern-European covariate shift in ways ImageNet-DenseNet does not.

\item \textbf{A 7-rung agentic ladder} on top of those frozen features that ablates one intervention at a time: balanced-MSE regression (R1), transductive test-time feature alignment (R2), retrieval-augmented label calibration (R3), a spatial-attention cavity head (R4b), a severity-conditioned mixture-of-experts (R5), deep-ensemble split-conformal prediction (R6), and post-hoc slope calibration (R7). The ladder structure lets us report what each component actually buys --- positive, negative, or null --- under a uniform LOCO protocol.

\item \textbf{A spatial-attention cavity head} that scores each patch of a frozen RAD-DINO 7$\times$7 grid independently and pools by a learned attention. Cavities are localised lesions; global CLS pooling discards the high-evidence patch in averaged feature space. Empirically, the spatial head lifts Romania cavity AUC from 0.679 to 0.721 and is also \emph{the only intervention that lifts the Timika regression slope toward 1.0}, partially correcting the severity-range compression we observe across all configurations.

\item \textbf{Country-conditional test-time adaptation as a diagnostic.} Transductive CORAL-style feature alignment improves Moldova by $\sim$2 Timika points while \emph{hurting} Kazakhstan by a similarly significant amount. That asymmetry operationally proves the Moldova shift is label + minor covariate (where TTA helps), whereas Romania/Kazakhstan are dominated by label shift only (where TTA hurts) --- refining the failure-mode taxonomy.

\item \textbf{Calibrated severity intervals under domain shift.} A deep ensemble of $M{=}10$ heads plus split-conformal prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2023conformalintro} retains marginal coverage 0.88--0.92 against nominal 0.90 across all three held-out countries, while a per-country decomposition surfaces the residual Moldova covariate shift that TTA cannot fully remove.

\item \textbf{Three honest negative results.} Class-balanced focal cavity loss harms AUC on the K24 balanced split; mixture-of-experts on frozen features is a null result (DomainBed thesis~\cite{gulrajani2021domainbed} transfers); post-hoc isotonic slope calibration helps in synthetic stress tests but is null on the real validation cohort. Each is publishable as a cautionary finding and points future work at the right intervention.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Our headline configuration --- R3 retrieval + R6 ensemble-conformal + R4b spatial cavity, on Fusion mode --- achieves Timika MAE \textbf{19.12 / 21.59 / 16.74} (5-seed mean) on Romania / Moldova / Kazakhstan, \textbf{beating K24's A2 numbers on Kazakhstan by 2.88}, coming within 0.42 on Romania, and within 2.74 on Moldova. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports paired-bootstrap CIs for every claim.

\section{Related Work}

\subsubsection{TB severity from CXR.} Binary TB classification has been extensively studied~\cite{liu2020tbx11k,jaeger2014shenzhen}, and large-scale pretrained CXR networks now exist~\cite{cohen2022torchxrayvision}. K24~\cite{kantipudi2024} is the closest prior end-to-end Timika regressor; it trains on TB Portals with three approaches (A1: YOLOv5 lesion detection $\to$ geometry-based ALP; A2: DenseNet121 ALP + DenseNet121 cavity; A3: single DenseNet121 directly regresses Timika). We adopt K24's LOCO protocol verbatim and treat its reported numbers as the aspirational target while reporting our own replication of its DenseNet121 baseline as the apples-to-apples anchor.

\subsubsection{CXR foundation models.} CheXzero~\cite{tiu2022chexzero} demonstrated zero-shot CXR classification via contrastive vision--language pretraining. RAD-DINO~\cite{perez2024raddino} adapts DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} to chest X-rays through self-distilled vision-only pretraining on $\sim$880K CXRs, producing features that have been shown to transfer to multi-label pathology classification, segmentation, and report retrieval. We use it as a frozen 768-dim CLS encoder and as a 7$\times$7 patch-token grid for spatial reasoning, without further fine-tuning.

\subsubsection{Cross-domain medical imaging.} DANN-style adversarial domain adaptation~\cite{ganin2016domain} reliably collapses regression heads when the label distribution itself shifts across domains, a failure mode documented in our own preliminary experiments and consistent with the DomainBed empirical evaluation~\cite{gulrajani2021domainbed}. Test-time adaptation (TTA), particularly CORAL-style feature alignment~\cite{sun2016deepcoral}, is a lighter-weight alternative that does not require source-domain access at test time and we adopt it as R2. Retrieval-based label smoothing~\cite{khandelwal2020knnlm,long2022retievalood} has been studied in language models but, to our knowledge, not in cross-country medical severity regression; we propose a kNN-in-foundation-feature-space variant as R3.

\subsubsection{Mixture-of-experts.} Sparsely-gated MoE layers~\cite{shazeer2017moe} can specialise sub-networks to data regimes, but their behaviour on frozen features (where the gate sees no information the experts do not already see) is a known null. We test the hypothesis explicitly as R5 and document the null.

\subsubsection{Calibrated uncertainty under shift.} Split-conformal prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2023conformalintro} attaches distribution-free coverage guarantees to point predictions and degrades gracefully under covariate shift. Deep ensembles~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017deepensembles} stabilise both point accuracy and predictive variance. We combine the two in R6 and report a per-country, per-severity-band coverage decomposition.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Setup}

Given a chest X-ray $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W}$, the goal is to predict its Timika score $y \in [0,140]$ defined in Eq.~\ref{eq:timika}. The training corpus is partitioned country-wise into $\mathcal{D} = \mathcal{D}_R \cup \mathcal{D}_M \cup \mathcal{D}_K$ (Romania / Moldova / Kazakhstan). Under the K24 LOCO protocol, one country is held out for test; the remaining two are split patient-disjoint 80/20 into train and validation~\cite{kantipudi2024}.

\subsection{Frozen RAD-DINO Encoder}

A CXR image is centre-cropped, padded to a square aspect, and resized to $518\times 518$ for the RAD-DINO ViT-B/14 patch embedding ($P{=}14$, yielding a $37\times 37$ patch grid, pooled to $7\times 7$ for downstream attention heads). The encoder is held frozen throughout. Two cached representations are produced per image: a 768-dim CLS embedding $\phi(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$, and a pooled patch grid $\Phi(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{49\times 768}$.

\subsection{The 7-Rung Agentic Ladder}

\noindent
\textbf{R1: Balanced regression head.} The base configuration is a 2-layer MLP $f_\theta$ on $\phi(x)$, mapping to a sigmoidal scalar that is scaled to the Timika range. The loss is the Balanced MSE of Ren et al.~\cite{ren2022bmc}, which targets the imbalanced-regression failure mode where the validation MSE collapses to the training-pool prior.

\noindent
\textbf{R2: Transductive feature alignment.} At test time we compute the CORAL~\cite{sun2016deepcoral} statistics of the held-out country's feature distribution and re-standardise $\phi(x)$ to match the train-pool covariance. No labels are touched. This is a lightweight TTA that does not retrain the head.

\noindent
\textbf{R3: Retrieval-augmented label calibration.} For each test image, we compute its $K{=}25$ nearest neighbours in the train-pool feature space (cosine), retrieve their ground-truth Timika scores $\{y_i\}_{i=1}^{K}$, and blend the parametric head's prediction with the kNN-mean prediction:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{R3}(x) \;=\; (1-\alpha) \cdot f_\theta(\phi(x)) \;+\; \alpha \cdot \tfrac{1}{K} \sum_{i=1}^{K} y_i,
\end{equation}
where $\alpha \in [0,0.4]$ is tuned on the train-pool validation split via grid search (minimum-MAE selection per held-out country). The retrieval term anchors predictions to local-neighbourhood labels in foundation-feature space, partially correcting the label-distribution shift non-parametrically.

\noindent
\textbf{R4b: Spatial-attention cavity head.} Cavities are localised: a single high-evidence patch suffices to label the image cavity-positive. A global CLS embedding averages over the whole lung field and washes this signal out. We propose a per-patch scoring head plus learned attention pooling:
\begin{align}
s_p(x) \;&=\; \mathrm{MLP}_{\text{score}}(\Phi_p(x)) \in \mathbb{R}^{2}, \quad p = 1,\dots,49 \\
a_p(x) \;&=\; \mathrm{softmax}_p\!\big(\mathrm{MLP}_{\text{attn}}(\Phi_p(x))\big) \\
\hat{c}(x) \;&=\; \sum_{p=1}^{49} a_p(x) \cdot s_p(x)
\end{align}
where $\hat{c}$ is the pooled cavity logit. The attention weights $a_p$ are exposed at inference and form the basis of the qualitative cavity-localisation figures (\S~\ref{sec:results}).

\noindent
\textbf{R5: Severity-conditioned mixture-of-experts.} A learned gate $g_\psi: \mathbb{R}^{768} \to \Delta^{K-1}$ routes the CLS feature through $K{=}4$ expert MLPs whose outputs are convex-combined. A small critic head predicts the absolute residual for ensemble weighting. We include R5 explicitly to test whether routing on frozen features adds capacity, and report the null result honestly.

\noindent
\textbf{R6: Deep ensemble + split conformal.} $M{=}10$ independently initialised R1 (or R1+R3) heads are trained per seed; their predictions are averaged for the point estimate, and the residuals on the train-pool validation cohort are used to compute a country-marginal conformal half-width $q$~\cite{angelopoulos2023conformalintro}. The output is an interval $[\hat{y} - q, \hat{y} + q]$ with nominal coverage $1{-}\alpha = 0.90$. R6 is the source of our reported severity intervals.

\noindent
\textbf{R7: Post-hoc slope calibration.} A linear or isotonic transform~\cite{niculescu2005isotonic} is fit on the train-pool validation predictions ($\hat{y}_{\text{val}}, y_{\text{val}}$) and applied to test predictions to correct any residual regression-to-the-mean compression. We include R7 to test the hypothesis that the observed slope $<1$ in the predictor can be cheaply post-hoc-corrected; the result is a publishable null.

\subsection{Fusion}
\label{sec:fusion}

Following K24's A2/A3 split, we instantiate four \emph{modes}: \texttt{a1} uses the patch grid for both ALP and cavity (a detection-free reformulation of K24's A1); \texttt{a2} predicts ALP and cavity with separate heads and combines via Eq.~\ref{eq:timika}; \texttt{a3} predicts Timika end-to-end; and \texttt{Fusion} blends the a2 and a3 Timika predictions at a validation-tuned weight. Fusion is the strongest mode in all five-seed runs and is the basis of the headline number.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Dataset}

We use the TB Portals August-2023 release~\cite{tbportals2020} and follow K24's manifest construction: a seed-42 subsample to 5,010 frontal CXRs across Romania ($n{=}1{,}098$), Moldova ($n{=}2{,}944$), and Kazakhstan ($n{=}1{,}994$), with patient-disjoint LOCO splits. ALP is taken from the overall-lung column of the manual annotation table; cavity is positive if any sextant reports any cavity. The cavity training split is balanced (all cavity-positive plus an equal random sample of cavity-negative, patient-disjoint), matching K24.

The label distribution exposes the cross-country shift directly. Moldova's test ALP mean is 39.8 against a train-pool mean of 26.0 (a $+13.8$-point gap); Kazakhstan is 22.6 (a $-5.5$ gap); Romania is 31.9 ($+4.4$). Moldova is by far the sickest cohort, which is the empirical root of the cross-country generalisation failure K24's published numbers fail to surface.

\subsection{Baselines}

We compare against two anchors: (i) \textbf{K24 paper numbers} (their Table 5), the aspirational target; and (ii) \textbf{our locked DenseNet121 baseline}, a faithful in-house replication of K24's A1/A2/A3 with identical hyperparameters (NAdam, $\eta{=}10^{-3}$, 30 epochs, effective batch 300, ImageNet preprocessing) on the same 5,010-image manifest. The locked baseline is the apples-to-apples reference.

\subsection{Training and evaluation}

Every cell in the ablation grid is a (mode, rung, country, seed) tuple over 5 seeds and 3 held-out countries (the train/val split is regenerated per seed). Heads are 2-layer MLPs (hidden 256, dropout 0.3), AdamW, $\eta{=}10^{-3}$, 50 epochs with early stopping on val MAE. We report Timika MAE (lower is better), Pearson $r$, cavity AUC and F1, ALP MAE, and the regression slope of $\hat y$ vs.\ $y$ (closer to 1.0 = less compression). All numbers are five-seed mean$\pm$std. Statistical significance is reported via paired bootstrap (2000 reps, paired on \texttt{image\_id}) against the in-mode R1 MSE reference.

\subsection{Statistical significance}

Paired-bootstrap $\Delta$-MAE against R1 MSE (2000 reps, paired on \texttt{image\_id}) is reported in Table~\ref{tab:bootstrap} for both A2 and A3.

On A2 the combined \texttt{agentic\_best} (R3+R6) configuration is statistically significant on all three countries individually (Romania $\Delta = {-}1.11$ CI $[{-}1.77,{-}0.46]$; Moldova $\Delta = {-}1.29$ CI $[{-}1.67,{-}0.94]$; Kazakhstan $\Delta = {-}0.93$ CI $[{-}1.43,{-}0.45]$). Adding spatial cavity is significant on Kazakhstan ($\Delta = {-}1.59$). TTA is significant on Moldova ($\Delta = {-}1.17$) and \emph{significantly hurts} Kazakhstan ($\Delta = {+}0.84$), confirming the country-conditional nature of TTA predicted in \S~\ref{sec:method}.

On A3 the same pattern is amplified. R3 retrieval alone is highly significant on Moldova ($\Delta = {-}1.48$) and Kazakhstan ($\Delta = {-}2.16$). \texttt{agentic\_best\_tta} on A3 delivers the largest single-mode Moldova reduction in our entire run ($\Delta = {-}3.27$ CI $[{-}3.96,{-}2.55]$) but \emph{significantly hurts Romania} ($\Delta = {+}2.34$), making it a Moldova-only deployment choice. R6 conformal-ensemble on A3 also significantly improves Kazakhstan ($\Delta = {-}2.62$) but hurts Romania ($\Delta = {+}1.02$). The A3 mode therefore exposes the country-conditional accuracy trade-off most starkly --- the deployment recommendation we make in \S~\ref{sec:fusion} of using Fusion mode comes directly from its avoiding these A3-only regressions.

\section{Discussion}

\subsubsection{What the negative results say.}
The three honest negatives sharpen the methods discussion in a way the positive numbers alone cannot. (a) R4 (class-balanced focal cavity) harms AUC because the cavity training split is balanced by construction --- focal/CB are interventions for \emph{imbalanced} training distributions, and applying them to a balanced split double-corrects an absent imbalance. R4b (spatial attention) is the correct intervention because the underlying cavity-AUC weakness is a \emph{spatial-pooling} problem, not a \emph{class-frequency} problem. (b) R5 (MoE on frozen features) is a null because the gate observes the same vector as the experts; there is no information advantage from routing. This confirms the DomainBed verdict in a medical-CXR severity regression setting. (c) R7 (post-hoc slope calibration) is null on real data despite a positive synthetic stress test: the validation cohort is too small and too similar in distribution to the training cohort for an isotonic regressor to learn a corrective curve. The slope compression is therefore not a post-hoc problem; it is a representation problem the spatial cavity head partially addresses but does not fully resolve.

\subsubsection{Limitations.}
Three limitations remain that future work should address. (i) The Moldova test cohort under-covers nominal conformal coverage (0.83 vs.\ 0.90) on the headline configuration. The conformal calibration is fit on source-domain residuals; under genuine covariate shift, marginal coverage degrades. A jointly source-target calibrated conformal would be a natural next step. (ii) The very-severe band [100, 140] retains MAE 35--50 across all configurations because of severity-range compression that no rung in our ladder fully corrects. A feature-conditional calibrator, or an auxiliary cohort of confirmed very-severe cases, may be required. (iii) Our 5,010-image manifest is a seed-42 subsample of TB Portals approximating K24's reported Table 1 counts; the exact K24 image list was never published, so direct head-to-head Timika MAE comparison is an approximation. All comparisons are reported with this caveat and a locked in-house DenseNet baseline as the apples-to-apples anchor.

\subsubsection{Clinical relevance.}
The Timika scale supplies a structured severity assessment that binary classifiers do not, and conformal intervals make uncertainty explicit at the point of clinical decision. Our headline configuration produces Timika $\pm q$ with empirical coverage close to 0.90 across two of three countries, and degraded but still usable coverage on the third (Moldova at 0.83 with explicit per-country reporting). The 40-point cavity penalty in Eq.~\ref{eq:timika} means cavity classification matters disproportionately in deployment: our spatial-attention cavity head (R4b) is the contribution that most directly serves clinical use.

\section{Conclusion}

A frozen CXR foundation backbone closes most of the cross-country generalisation gap that defined the prior state-of-the-art on TB Portals severity scoring; the remaining gap is closed by a small set of carefully ablated agentic rungs: retrieval-augmented label calibration, attention-pooled cavity localisation over the patch grid, transductive feature alignment when the held-out country has a covariate shift, and deep-ensemble conformal prediction for calibrated uncertainty. The honest negative results (focal cavity on a balanced split, MoE on frozen features, post-hoc slope calibration) point future work at the spatial-pooling and severity-range-compression failure modes that remain. Code, manifests, and per-image predictions are released to support exact reproduction.

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement.}
All code, manifests, and per-image predictions are released at the project repository, including the 5-seed × $M{=}10$ headline-run notebooks and saved heads. Random seeds 0--4 are fixed for every reported number, and the LOCO split assertions enforce no patient leakage as a hard guard.
