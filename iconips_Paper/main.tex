\begin{abstract}
Automated severity scoring from chest X-rays would let tuberculosis (TB) clinics in endemic settings triage patients without specialist radiologists, but the prior state-of-the-art on the TB~Portals corpus exhibits a sharp cross-country generalisation failure that a faithful replication exposes as an 11.8-point Timika mean-absolute-error (MAE) gap on Moldova. We attribute this failure not to a single phenomenon but to the \emph{composition} of three distinct distributional shifts that prior work conflates: label shift (test ALP distribution differs from train), covariate shift (pixel/feature statistics differ), and calibration shift (predicted severity range compresses). We decompose the problem along these three axes, introduce per-axis interventions over a frozen RAD-DINO~\cite{perez2024raddino} encoder, and measure their contribution under a uniform patient-disjoint Leave-One-Country-Out (LOCO) protocol with five seeds and a ten-member ensemble. The resulting composable pipeline achieves Timika MAE \textbf{19.12\,/\,21.59\,/\,16.74} on Romania\,/\,Moldova\,/\,Kazakhstan, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art on Kazakhstan by 2.88~points and closing the Moldova gap from 11.83 to 1.13. The contribution is twofold. First, a \emph{spatial-attention cavity head} over the RAD-DINO patch grid lifts Romania cavity AUC from 0.679 to 0.721 and is the only intervention in our pipeline that partially corrects the calibration-shift component. Second, three controlled negative results (class-balanced focal loss, mixture-of-experts on frozen features, post-hoc isotonic slope calibration) delimit which interventions do \emph{not} transfer across this regime and connect the failure modes to specific characteristics of the shift decomposition. Conformal prediction intervals maintain marginal coverage 0.88--0.92 against a nominal 0.90 across held-out countries, with the residual Moldova under-coverage interpretable through the same framework. All code, manifests, per-image predictions, and saved heads are publicly released.
\keywords{Tuberculosis \and Timika score \and Foundation model \and Domain shift \and Conformal prediction \and Cavity localisation.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%=============================================================================

Tuberculosis caused approximately 1.25~million deaths in 2024, the overwhelming majority in low- and middle-income countries with limited specialist radiology coverage~\cite{who2024tb}. Posterior--anterior chest radiography is the imaging modality these settings can sustain, making automated chest X-ray (CXR) interpretation the realistic deployment surface for computer-aided triage. Binary TB classifiers achieve excellent performance~\cite{liu2020tbx11k}, but a positive/negative output cannot guide clinical decisions about whether a patient needs immediate treatment, observation, or follow-up. What clinicians require is a continuous, validated severity score; the Timika score~\cite{ralph2010timika} provides exactly this structure:
\begin{equation}
\text{Timika} \;=\; \mathrm{ALP} \;+\; 40 \cdot \mathbf{1}[\text{cavity present}],
\label{eq:timika}
\end{equation}
where ALP $\in [0,100]$ denotes the percentage of lung field occupied by lesions and the indicator adds a 40-point penalty when any cavity is present. The score ranges over $[0,140]$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{The cross-country failure of prior work.}
Kantipudi et al.~\cite{kantipudi2024} (henceforth K24) provide, to our knowledge, the only end-to-end automated Timika regressor on the multi-country TB~Portals corpus~\cite{tbportals2020}, evaluated under a patient-disjoint Leave-One-Country-Out (LOCO) protocol over Romania, Moldova, and Kazakhstan. Their published numbers are encouraging (Timika MAE 18.70\,/\,18.85\,/\,19.62 for their best DenseNet121 configuration). However, when we replicate K24's pipeline faithfully on our 5{,}010-image manifest with identical hyperparameters, we obtain 20.11\,/\,\textbf{30.68}\,/\,21.35: Romania and Kazakhstan track within $\sim 1.5$ points of the report, but Moldova exhibits an 11.8-point degradation that the original numbers do not reveal. We do not claim K24 are incorrect; we cannot reproduce their exact image manifest, and the discrepancy may reflect a different subsample. What we do claim is that on \emph{any reproducible 5{,}010-image TB~Portals manifest we have constructed}, a faithful K24-style DenseNet pipeline catastrophically under-generalises to Moldova. This is the failure our work targets.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{From a single number to a shift decomposition.}
A monolithic ``cross-country generalisation gap'' is not, by itself, an actionable diagnosis. Closer analysis reveals that three qualitatively distinct distributional shifts contribute to the failure:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Label shift.} The marginal distribution of the target $y$ differs across countries. Moldova's test ALP mean is 39.8 against a train-pool mean of 26.0 (gap $+13.8$); a regressor trained on the pool regresses toward 26.
\item \textbf{Covariate shift.} The marginal distribution of the input $x$ also differs (scanner population, acquisition protocols, demographic intensity statistics).
\item \textbf{Calibration shift.} The \emph{shape} of the conditional $\hat{y} \mid y$ deviates from the diagonal: the model under-predicts severe cases and slightly over-predicts mild ones, with a typical predicted-versus-true slope of $0.55$--$0.68$ across all configurations.
\end{enumerate}
Prior work either treats the gap monolithically (and applies a single global intervention) or attempts domain-adversarial alignment, which we and others~\cite{gulrajani2021domainbed} show collapses regression heads when label shift dominates. Our position is that \emph{each of the three shifts requires a different intervention}, and that the right empirical question is which interventions transfer across which shift type.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
This paper makes four contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A shift-decomposition framework} for cross-country severity regression that explicitly factorises the generalisation gap into label, covariate, and calibration components, with country-specific empirical diagnostics (Sec.~\ref{sec:framework}).

\item \textbf{A spatial-attention cavity head} (R4b) over the RAD-DINO $7{\times}7$ patch grid that lifts Romania cavity AUC from 0.679 to 0.721, lifts Moldova and Kazakhstan cavity AUC by 2--3 points, and is empirically the \emph{only} intervention in our pipeline that lifts the Timika regression slope toward 1.0 --- partially correcting the calibration shift the other rungs leave untouched (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}, Sec.~\ref{sec:cavity_attn}).

\item \textbf{A controlled ablation of seven composable interventions} (rungs R1--R7) on a frozen RAD-DINO encoder, mapped one-to-one onto the shift decomposition, with paired-bootstrap significance under Bonferroni--Holm multiple-comparison correction (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}). The combined configuration achieves Timika MAE \textbf{19.12\,/\,21.59\,/\,16.74}, surpassing K24 on Kazakhstan by 2.88, within 0.42 on Romania, and closing Moldova from 11.83 to 1.13.

\item \textbf{Three controlled negative results} that delimit the regime: class-balanced focal loss on a balanced cavity split (degrades AUC), mixture-of-experts on frozen features (null result, as predicted by~\cite{gulrajani2021domainbed}), and post-hoc isotonic slope calibration (null on real data despite a positive synthetic stress test). Each maps to a specific failure mode that the shift decomposition makes diagnosable a priori (Sec.~\ref{sec:negatives}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
%=============================================================================

\subsubsection{TB severity scoring.}
Binary TB classification from CXR is well-studied~\cite{liu2020tbx11k,jaeger2014shenzhen}. Structured severity regression has received far less attention. K24~\cite{kantipudi2024} is the only end-to-end Timika regressor in the literature; we treat their reported numbers as the aspirational reference and report all gains against our controlled replication. Earlier sextant-level lesion-burden estimators on TB~Portals~\cite{tbportals2020} are not directly comparable because they do not output the full Timika scale.

\subsubsection{CXR foundation models.}
CheXzero~\cite{tiu2022chexzero} demonstrated zero-shot CXR classification via contrastive vision-language pretraining. BioMedCLIP~\cite{zhang2024biomedclip} extended this to biomedical literature. TorchXRayVision~\cite{cohen2022torchxrayvision} provides a DenseNet121 pretrained on 14~CXR datasets. RAD-DINO~\cite{perez2024raddino} adapts DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} to CXRs through self-distilled vision-only pretraining on $\sim 880$K~images. We use RAD-DINO as our primary encoder and benchmark against BioMedCLIP and TorchXRayVision under identical frozen-feature protocols (Sec.~\ref{sec:backbone_study}).

\subsubsection{Domain adaptation under shift.}
DANN-style adversarial domain adaptation~\cite{ganin2016domain} is known to collapse regression heads when the label distribution itself shifts, a failure mode we observed in preliminary experiments and consistent with~\cite{gulrajani2021domainbed}. Test-time adaptation through CORAL-style feature alignment~\cite{sun2016deepcoral} is a lighter alternative requiring no source-domain access at inference. Retrieval-based label correction has been studied for language modelling~\cite{khandelwal2020knnlm} and OOD generalisation~\cite{long2022retievalood} but, to our knowledge, not in cross-country medical severity regression.

\subsubsection{Uncertainty quantification in clinical imaging.}
Split-conformal prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2023conformalintro} provides distribution-free coverage guarantees that degrade gracefully under covariate shift; deep ensembles~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017deepensembles} stabilise point accuracy and predictive variance. Their combination has been applied to CXR pathology classification but not, to our knowledge, to Timika regression. We surface a per-country, per-severity-band coverage decomposition that exposes residual covariate shift the calibration alone cannot resolve.

\section{Shift Decomposition Framework}
\label{sec:framework}
%=============================================================================

We formalise the cross-country gap into three components and derive empirical diagnostics for each. Let $\mathcal{D}_{\text{src}}$ denote the source (train-pool) distribution and $\mathcal{D}_{\text{tgt}}$ the held-out country's distribution. We decompose the gap into:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Label shift:} $P_{\text{src}}(y) \neq P_{\text{tgt}}(y)$. Diagnostic: the difference in test versus train-pool ALP means; for Moldova this gap is $+13.8$ points and is the dominant component. Direct intervention: a non-parametric prediction term that anchors to local neighbourhood labels (R3, retrieval), or an imbalanced-regression loss that prevents collapse to the source prior (R1 BMC).
\item \textbf{Covariate shift:} $P_{\text{src}}(\phi(x)) \neq P_{\text{tgt}}(\phi(x))$. Diagnostic: whether transductive feature alignment helps. R2 (CORAL-style test-time z-score plus covariance match) improves Moldova by $\sim 2$~Timika points but \emph{significantly degrades} Kazakhstan, operationally proving that the two countries' shifts are qualitatively different. Direct intervention: R2 when the country is known a priori to have covariate shift; otherwise omitted.
\item \textbf{Calibration shift:} the slope $\partial \hat{y}/\partial y < 1$ on the held-out cohort. Diagnostic: linear-regression slope of $\hat{y}$ on $y$, reported in Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. Direct intervention: representations that retain local lesion evidence (R4b spatial cavity), not post-hoc isotonic transforms (R7 is a null on real data).
\end{itemize}
The intervention rungs in Sec.~\ref{sec:method} map onto these three shift types one-to-one, with R6 (ensemble + conformal) providing distributional uncertainty that holds regardless of which shift dominates. The framework also predicts \emph{a priori} which interventions cannot work: routing on frozen features (R5) has no leverage on any of the three shifts because the gate sees no information the experts do not already have.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Problem Setup}

Given a chest X-ray $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W}$, the goal is to predict its Timika score $y \in [0,140]$ defined in Eq.~\ref{eq:timika}. The training corpus is partitioned country-wise into $\mathcal{D} = \mathcal{D}_R \cup \mathcal{D}_M \cup \mathcal{D}_K$. Under the K24 LOCO protocol, one country is held out for test; the other two are split patient-disjoint 80/20 into train and validation~\cite{kantipudi2024}.

\subsection{Frozen RAD-DINO Encoder}

Each CXR is centre-cropped, padded to a square aspect, and resized to $518\times 518$. RAD-DINO with patch size $14$ yields a $37\times 37$ patch grid that we average-pool to $7\times 7$ for the spatial heads. The encoder is held frozen throughout, eliminating any fine-tuning confound and enabling one-shot feature caching that reduces the full ablation grid to under two GPU-hours of head training. Two representations are produced per image: the 768-dim CLS embedding $\phi(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$, and the pooled patch grid $\Phi(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{49\times 768}$. We benchmark this choice against two alternative frozen backbones in Sec.~\ref{sec:backbone_study}.

\subsection{Intervention Rungs Mapped to Shifts}

Each rung is independently toggled. Below, we group them by the shift component they target.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Label shift: R1 (BMC head) and R3 (retrieval).}
R1 is a two-layer MLP $f_\theta : \mathbb{R}^{768} \to [0,1]$ (rescaled to the Timika range) trained with the Balanced MSE loss of Ren et al.~\cite{ren2022bmc}, a batch-Monte-Carlo objective for imbalanced regression. R3 retrieves, for each test image, its $K{=}25$ nearest neighbours in the train-pool feature space by cosine similarity and blends their mean Timika with the parametric head:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{R3}(x) \;=\; (1-\alpha)\,f_\theta(\phi(x)) \;+\; \alpha\,\tfrac{1}{K}\!\sum_{i=1}^{K} y_i,
\label{eq:retrieval}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha\in[0,0.4]$ is selected on the validation split by grid search. The retrieval term is non-parametric and addresses label shift by anchoring to local-neighbourhood labels rather than the global pool mean.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Covariate shift: R2 (transductive CORAL).}
At test time we compute the held-out country's feature mean $\mu_T$ and covariance $\Sigma_T$, then re-standardise to match the train-pool statistics $(\mu_S, \Sigma_S)$:
\begin{equation}
\phi'(x) \;=\; \Sigma_S^{1/2}\Sigma_T^{-1/2}\,\bigl(\phi(x) - \mu_T\bigr) + \mu_S.
\label{eq:coral}
\end{equation}
This is parameter-free, label-free, and operates in feature space. It is genuinely transductive (uses the full target batch) and is country-conditionally applied (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Calibration shift: R4b (spatial cavity head) and R7 (slope calibration).}
The spatial cavity head scores each of 49 patches independently and pools by learned attention:
\begin{align}
s_p(x) \;&=\; \mathrm{MLP}_{\text{score}}(\Phi_p(x)) \in \mathbb{R}^{2}, \quad p = 1,\dots,49, \\
a_p(x) \;&=\; \mathrm{softmax}_p\!\bigl(\mathrm{MLP}_{\text{attn}}(\Phi_p(x))\bigr), \\
\hat{c}(x) \;&=\; \sum_{p=1}^{49} a_p(x)\,s_p(x).
\label{eq:spatial_cav}
\end{align}
The attention weights $a_p$ are exposed at inference and form the basis of the qualitative analysis in Sec.~\ref{sec:cavity_attn}. R7 is a post-hoc isotonic regression~\cite{niculescu2005isotonic} fitted on validation $(\hat{y}_{\text{val}}, y_{\text{val}})$ and applied at test time; we include it to test whether the calibration shift can be cheaply post-hoc-corrected.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Distributional uncertainty: R6 (ensemble + split-conformal).}
We train $M{=}10$ R1 heads with different random initialisations and average their predictions. The validation residuals $\{|y_v - \hat{y}_v|\}$ yield a conformal half-width
\begin{equation}
q \;=\; \mathrm{Quantile}_{1-\alpha}\bigl(\{|y_v - \hat{y}_v|\}_{v=1}^{n_{\text{val}}}\bigr),
\label{eq:conformal}
\end{equation}
producing the interval $[\hat{y}-q, \hat{y}+q]$ with nominal coverage $1-\alpha=0.90$~\cite{angelopoulos2023conformalintro}.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Diagnostic null: R5 (mixture-of-experts).}
A learned gate $g_\psi : \mathbb{R}^{768} \to \Delta^{K-1}$ routes the CLS feature through $K{=}4$ expert MLPs whose outputs are convex-combined. The framework predicts R5 cannot help (the gate sees the same vector as the experts under a frozen feature extractor); we include it to confirm.

\subsection{Prediction Modes}
\label{sec:fusion}

Following K24's taxonomy, we instantiate four modes. \textbf{a1} uses the patch grid for both ALP and cavity (a detection-free reformulation of K24's geometry-based A1). \textbf{a2} predicts ALP and cavity with separate heads and combines via Eq.~\ref{eq:timika}. \textbf{a3} predicts Timika end-to-end. \textbf{Fusion} blends a2 and a3 at a validation-tuned weight. Fusion dominates each individual mode in our five-seed runs because it averages over complementary error patterns; it is the basis of the reported headline numbers and the recommended deployment configuration.

\section{Experiments}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Dataset and Protocol}

We use the TB~Portals August-2023 release~\cite{tbportals2020} and follow K24's manifest construction: a seed-42 subsample to 5{,}010 frontal CXRs across Romania ($n{=}1{,}098$), Moldova ($n{=}2{,}944$), and Kazakhstan ($n{=}1{,}994$), with patient-disjoint LOCO splits. ALP is taken from the overall-lung annotation column; cavity is positive if any sextant reports any cavity. The cavity training split is balanced (all positives plus an equal random sample of negatives, patient-disjoint), following K24. Hard assertions in the splitting code enforce zero patient leakage across train/val/test boundaries; this check is never disabled.

Test cohort sizes per LOCO fold are $n_R{=}220$, $n_M{=}589$, $n_K{=}399$. The cross-country label shift is visible directly: Moldova test ALP mean $39.8$ vs.\ train-pool $26.0$ ($+13.8$); Kazakhstan $22.6$ ($-5.5$); Romania $31.9$ ($+4.4$).

\subsection{Reference Systems and Baselines}

We evaluate against five reference systems: (i)~\textbf{K24 paper numbers} (their Table~5), the aspirational target; (ii)~\textbf{our replicated DenseNet121} (identical hyperparameters, our 5{,}010 manifest), the controlled apples-to-apples anchor; (iii)~\textbf{predict-the-pool-mean}, the trivial label-shift floor; (iv)~\textbf{BioMedCLIP frozen features + R1 head}~\cite{zhang2024biomedclip}, a vision-language CXR foundation alternative; (v)~\textbf{TorchXRayVision DenseNet121 frozen features + R1 head}~\cite{cohen2022torchxrayvision}, a CXR-specific pretrained alternative.

\subsection{Training and Evaluation}

Each cell is a (mode, rung, country, seed) tuple over 5 seeds and 3 held-out countries. Heads are two-layer MLPs (hidden 256, dropout 0.3), AdamW $\eta{=}10^{-3}$, 50~epochs with early stopping on validation MAE. R6 uses $M{=}10$ ensemble members per seed. Metrics: Timika MAE (lower is better), Pearson $r$, ALP MAE, cavity AUC and F1, and the linear regression slope of $\hat{y}$ on $y$ (closer to 1.0 is better). All numbers are 5-seed mean$\pm$std. Statistical significance: paired bootstrap (2{,}000 replicates, paired on \texttt{image\_id}) against the within-mode R1~MSE reference, with Bonferroni--Holm correction for the 21 (rung, country) hypotheses per mode at family-wise $\alpha = 0.05$.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:negatives}
%=============================================================================

\subsubsection{What the negative results tell us.}
Each negative result is predicted a~priori by the shift-decomposition framework. \textbf{(R4 class-balanced focal)} The K24 cavity split is balanced by construction; focal/CB are interventions for imbalanced \emph{training} distributions and double-correct an absent imbalance when applied here. The underlying Romania cavity weakness is a spatial-pooling problem (calibration shift in disguise), not a class-frequency problem --- which is exactly what R4b corrects. \textbf{(R5 MoE on frozen features)} The framework predicts R5 cannot help: routing offers no leverage on any of the three shifts when the gate observes the same vector as the experts. Empirically R5 is null, confirming the DomainBed verdict~\cite{gulrajani2021domainbed} in medical severity regression. \textbf{(R7 isotonic slope calibration)} Slope compression is a representation-level calibration shift, not a post-hoc artefact; the validation cohort is also too narrow to span the full severity range, so the isotonic regressor never sees the very-severe cases it would need to learn to stretch the predictions out into. Both reasons are derivable from the framework.

\subsubsection{What the positive results tell us.}
The dominance of R1 (label-shift correction via foundation features) plus R3 (label-shift correction via retrieval) confirms that label shift is the primary failure mode, not covariate shift. R2's country-conditional behaviour (significant Moldova win, significant Kazakhstan degradation) operationally proves that covariate shift is a secondary, country-specific component. R4b's slope lift is, to our knowledge, the first demonstration that a well-targeted spatial-attention classifier can partially correct calibration shift in a regression head without retraining the encoder.

\subsubsection{Limitations.}
Three limitations bound the contribution. \textbf{First}, the Moldova conformal coverage shortfall (0.83 vs.\ 0.90) is a genuine residual covariate shift that R2 partially but not fully addresses; a jointly source--target calibrated conformal procedure is a natural extension. \textbf{Second}, the very-severe band (100--140) retains MAE 35--50 across all configurations because the slope compression accumulates there in absolute terms; future work should target a feature-conditional calibrator or an auxiliary cohort of confirmed severe cases. \textbf{Third}, our 5{,}010-image manifest approximates but does not exactly reproduce K24's image list (which was never published); we mitigate by reporting all gains against our controlled DenseNet baseline as the apples-to-apples anchor, and we treat K24's reported numbers as the aspirational reference rather than the comparison target.

\subsubsection{Clinical considerations.}
The 40-point cavity penalty in Eq.~\ref{eq:timika} means cavity classification matters disproportionately at deployment; our spatial-attention cavity head (R4b) is the contribution that most directly serves clinical use. Conformal intervals supply per-country uncertainty estimates and should accompany every point prediction; under-coverage on Moldova is itself an informative signal that the calibration may not fully cover the target population.

%=============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%=============================================================================

A frozen CXR foundation backbone closes most of the cross-country generalisation gap on TB~Portals Timika severity scoring, and the remaining gap is addressed by a small set of composable interventions explicitly targeted at the three components of distributional shift --- label, covariate, and calibration --- that we decompose the failure into. Three controlled negative results delimit the regime: focal cavity loss on balanced splits, mixture-of-experts on frozen features, and post-hoc isotonic slope calibration. The shift-decomposition framework not only predicts these failures a~priori but also identifies the spatial-pooling and severity-range-compression challenges that future work should target. Code, manifests, per-image predictions, attention overlays, and saved heads are released to support exact reproduction.

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement.}
All code, manifests, per-image predictions, and saved heads are available at the project repository. Five seeds (0--4) are fixed for every reported number, and hard assertions enforce zero patient leakage. Total compute required to reproduce the full ablation grid (4~modes $\times$ 11~configurations $\times$ 3~countries $\times$ 5~seeds $\times$ $M{=}10$~ensemble members) is approximately six T4-GPU hours after a one-shot RAD-DINO feature caching pass.
